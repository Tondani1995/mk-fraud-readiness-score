# Depth by information gain, re-ranking and consistency checks (generated)

> HYPOTHETICAL: research simulation, not an approved or production value. Generated by `scripts/research/depth/information-gain.mjs` through the session layer with review engine build 1.6, bank 1.3-review and coverage map 1.3. Fictional organisations only. P6c is strong everywhere except the practices of the objectives no confirmation covers.

## 1. How build 1.6 chooses depth questions

Every candidate is valued by what its answer could change, never by its place in a ranking of findings. A coverage question (the only question on an objective no confirmation covers) starts at 3; a diagnostic of a partly reported control at 2; a follow-up at 2 where the confirmation is partly in place and 1 where it is not in place (what else exists matters less than the gap itself). Tier 1 adds 1 and tier 2 adds 0.5; a question other practices depend on adds 1, because its answer decides what is asked next; a question on an objective with no known answer adds 1, and a detail question on an objective already answered twice loses 1. Two further rules follow the existing relationships and the answers already given: a scenario whose confirmations are reported in place is investigated when one of its supporting practices has already been reported as a gap (a consistency check or a function's answer), and a question that cannot yet be asked because a fact decides whether it applies is replaced by that fact, valued at the best question it would open less 0.5. Questions at 3 or more are recommended, at most 15 per function, 4 diagnostics per scenario and 2 coverage questions per objective. The selection is recomputed on every send, so each round of answers changes the next.

## 2. Information gain against the earlier priority-eight selection

Both methods start from the same session after the respondent interview and the functions' answers to "I don't know" and delegated areas. The priority-eight method sends one round: questions only for the eight gaps ranked first by R1 before any depth answer, plus up to two coverage questions per objective. Information gain sends recommended rounds until nothing further qualifies.

"Gaps behind confirmations in place" are answers reporting a supporting practice not in place, partly in place or set out only, on a scenario whose confirmations were all reported in place. They are where a self-reported confirmation and the organisation's own further answers disagree. The priority-eight method reaches them only through its coverage questions, because a scenario whose confirmations are in place never enters its eight. Gaps found under a control already reported not in place repeat what is known; the priority-eight method finds more of those (it asks two follow-ups per gap whatever the answer), which is why it can find more gaps in total for some profiles while adding less to any conclusion.

Where information gain found fewer gaps (P4: 5 against 14), its threshold left out diagnostics of tier 2 and tier 3 scenarios whose objective already has an answer, which for an organisation with few applicable confirmations are most of the gaps that remain. Whether the threshold or the limits should relax when few confirmations apply is a burden decision for MK; it is recorded in the decision register.

## 3. Do further answers change which issues deserve attention?

Ranking is an open MK decision, so the test uses two candidate rankings. R1 uses confirmation answers only. R2 counts every answer: the worst confirmation, the gaps among the scenario's supporting practices and the tier. "Entered" counts the issues in the top eight after the depth answers that were not in the top eight before them.

Reading the table: under R1 the top eight never changes in these runs, because R1 looks only at confirmations and every confirmation was answered before depth began; measuring depth against the eight chosen before it is circular. Under R2 the depth answers change the top eight for 6 of 7 profiles (P1, P2, P3, P5, P6b, P6c). Information gain changes it by more issues than the priority-eight method for P1, P2, P3, P5, by the same number for P6c and by fewer for P4, P6b. Where the priority-eight method moves the ranking as much, its first eight already pointed at the weak scenarios; it still cannot look beyond them except through coverage questions.

## 5. Consistency checks per profile

The rule (deterministic and applicability-aware): after a tier 1 or tier 2 confirmation is reported fully in place, the first of its follow-ups that applies and has not been asked is asked straight away, at most one per area and ten in all, in the order the confirmations were answered. The population is every tier 1 and tier 2 confirmation reported fully in place that has at least one follow-up in scope. A check is counted as tested only when its follow-up has an answer other than "I don't know".

What a check is and is not. Each check is one further self-reported answer about a practice that supports a confirmation the organisation reported in place. It is not a test of operation and it is not evidence; evidence review belongs to Advisory. "Not tested" counts the confirmations in the population that were not selected: nothing is known about their consistency, and no report may say otherwise. A conflicting answer is a disagreement between two answers, which may mean the confirmation is overstated or that the follow-up was read more strictly; narrower scope and partial operation are not contradictions. No score, band or cap is changed by any outcome: F2 (score ceiling) and F4 (band limit) stay inactive until MK approves their definitions and proportionality.

P6b and P6c show why the checks matter. Their scoring frames are identical (every confirmation reported in place), but P6b's checks find 6 conflicting answers and P6c's find 1: only the checks distinguish an overstated confirmation from weaknesses that sit where no confirmation looks.
