# Formal MK quality review: the 34 owner-review dimensions

Reporting Bible v1.1, section 22. The review covers the actual manuscripts (`report-excellence/<org>/*-manuscript.txt`) and the actual PDFs for all four fictional organisations, read page by page. Scores are out of 10 and are given separately for Essential (E) and Comprehensive (C). Each dimension shows the previous live reports (Vhutshilo V10 Essential and Motheo V3 Comprehensive), the revised prototype at `55ea176`, and the improved reports. The release threshold is 9.5 on every material dimension, and all 34 are treated as material. No score is averaged, and automated test passes are not counted as quality.

**Verdict: not releasable.** No improved report reaches 9.5 on every dimension. Three conditions outside the reports' own quality hold several dimensions down whatever the prose achieves:

- the production AI manuscript stage has not run (AT-AI-01 blocked);
- MK has not approved a readiness method or a decision method;
- the control, pathway and decision content is draft.

Within those limits, the remaining gaps that this workstream controls are Comprehensive depth, South African context and owner specificity. These are set out under each dimension.

Scale: 10 means MK would send the page to a board without changing a word. 9.5 is the release floor. 8 means sound but visibly short of the standard. 7 is MK's assessment of the previous live reports.

### 1. Immediate buyer value
**Standard:** a reader sees on the first pages what MK concluded and what to do (Bible 10.1, 13.1).
**Evidence:** P6b Essential pp. 3 to 4 state the strong-on-the-surface, uneven-underneath position, the three patterns and the four first controls by name. P3 Comprehensive pp. 2 to 3 explain why the framework sits at the centre and not in operations, how the themes interact, and the twelve-month path.
**Scores:** live E 7.0, C 7.5 · prototype E 5.5, C 6.0 · **improved E 8.8, C 8.6**.
**Deficiencies:** the conclusion slot reads "None issued"; the prototype notice and fixture tag remind the buyer that the product is unfinished.
**Corrective action:** approve the readiness method (D-1) so that the conclusion slot carries MK's conclusion; run AT-AI-01.

### 2. Executive readability
**Standard:** answer-first pages; no questionnaire interpretation needed (Bible 19.2, 13.1).
**Evidence:** every page opens with a claim heading. The executive summary is four to seven connected paragraphs. Finding strips carry four short fields, not database cards.
**Scores:** live E 6.5, C 8.0 · prototype E 6.5, C 5.0 · **improved E 9.2, C 8.7**.
**Deficiencies:** the Comprehensive decision-option tables (five columns at 7.6 pt) and blueprint panels are dense for a board reader.
**Corrective action:** cut each decision table to the options and trade-off, and move effort and benefit into the workbook; raise panel text to 8 pt.

### 3. Narrative flow
**Standard:** connected movements; transitions explain why the reader moves on (Bible 7.2).
**Evidence:** each movement opens with a transition, for example the move from findings to pathways: "The findings do not operate independently…".
**Scores:** live E 7.0, C 8.0 · prototype E 4.0, C 4.5 · **improved E 9.2, C 8.8**.
**Deficiencies:** in the Comprehensive, the sequence of blueprint, operating model, decisions, programme and scorecard reads as a series of exhibits with short bridges.
**Corrective action:** give each design section a two-sentence bridge that names what the previous section left unresolved (writer-brief requirement for AT-AI-01).

### 4. Quality of executive diagnosis

**Scores:** live E 7.0, C 8.0 · prototype E 5.0, C 5.5 · **improved E 9.0, C 9.0**.

### 6. Fraud-specific insight
**Standard:** explains how fraud arises across boundaries and how controls interact (refined brief, item 6).
**Evidence:**
- A blind count cannot reveal a loss without an expected quantity (P6b).
- AI defeats controls that rely on recognition (P6b).
- A changed supplier record diverts every later payment until the supplier complains (P3).
- Two authorisers and a call-back protect different steps (P3, P5).
**Scores:** live E 6.5, C 7.0 · prototype E 5.5, C 6.0 · **improved E 9.2, C 9.2**.
**Deficiencies:** few insights on detection economics (time to discovery, who usually finds the loss) beyond the pathways.
**Corrective action:** add a "how this is usually discovered" element to the pathway structures (scenario-structures 0.2).

### 7. Cross-domain synthesis
**Standard:** themes that draw findings together across areas (Bible 11.1, 13.2).
**Evidence:**
- Themes are built from the method's cross-area objectives, for example verification of changes across suppliers, payroll, customers and digital in P3 and P5.
- The Comprehensive's theme map shows where each theme appears.
- The Comprehensive explains how the themes interact.
**Scores:** live E 7.0, C 8.0 · prototype E 5.0, C 6.5 · **improved E 9.0, C 9.1**.
**Deficiencies:** P4 has three themes for a small firm, one of them thin.
**Corrective action:** allow two themes when the method supports no more than two (story-plan rule).

### 8. Finding quality
**Standard:** advisory observations with claim heading, recorded position, interpretation, fraud implication and management implication (Bible 11.2, 13.3).
**Evidence:** P6b's seven findings each follow that construction, without repeating the recorded sentence.
**Scores:** live E 6.0, C 7.5 · prototype E 5.5, C 6.0 · **improved E 9.1, C 8.6**.
**Deficiencies:** the Comprehensive findings are no deeper than the Essential's. They are similar in length (for example P6b, 1,139 words against 1,198) and add control-system context, but rarely a separate management consequence.
**Corrective action:** set a Comprehensive finding construction with a required "how it interacts with other controls" and a required "management consequence" paragraph, and roughly 1.5 times the Essential length (writer brief and validator movement check).

### 9. Scenario realism
**Standard:** actor, opportunity, entry, mechanism, bypass, concealment, consequence, warning signs, containment and long-term response, written as a story (Bible 11.3).
**Evidence:** the pathway structures carry all ten parts. The prose names the actor class and the concealment, for example the overbilling pathway in P6b.
**Scores:** live E 6.5, C 7.0 · prototype E 5.0, C 5.0 · **improved E 9.0, C 9.0**.
**Deficiencies:** the structures are draft content; some warning-sign lists are generic across organisations.
**Corrective action:** D-8; tailor warning signs by operating context (industry, cash, stock).

### 10. Risk reasoning
**Standard:** why a weakness creates exposure, without unsupported ratings.
**Evidence:** reasoning runs through mechanism and consequence, and no likelihood or impact rating is given. The workbook's Risk Register says "Not rated".
**Scores:** live E 6.0, C 6.5 · prototype E 7.5, C 7.5 · **improved E 8.6, C 8.6**.
**Deficiencies:** without an approved method, the reports cannot say which exposure is larger, which a CFO will ask.
**Corrective action:** D-3. Meanwhile, state the explicit factors (money, cash or stock leaving; volume; incident history) for each first response in the PDF, not only in the workbook.

### 11. Control-design specificity
**Standard:** target practice, owner, population, frequency, record, independent check, escalation, service level, measure, failure response, dependencies (Bible 13.4).
**Evidence:** PDF blueprint panels for five controls; the workbook gives every control all twelve fields. There is no generic measure (library 0.1).
**Scores:** live E 7.0, C 7.5 · prototype E 8.0, C 8.0 · **improved E 9.1, C 9.2**.
**Deficiencies:**
- service levels are proposals;
- the independent check falls back to "a periodic review by the fraud risk lead" for many archetypes;
- a few workbook populations fall back to a generic phrase where the library has no population.
**Corrective action:** add an independent-check and population field to every library entry (library 0.2); D-5.

### 12. Implementation practicality
**Standard:** a sequenced plan with owners, deliverables and outcomes, not walls of text (Bible 10, 13.7).
**Evidence:**
- The Essential's 30/60/90 board shows outcomes and owners per stage.
- The Comprehensive's twelve-month board runs Stabilise, Establish, Embed, Mature.
- The workbook holds every action with a deliverable and completion test.
**Scores:** live E 7.0, C 7.5 · prototype E 6.5, C 7.0 · **improved E 8.9, C 8.9**.
**Deficiencies:**
- "Name the executive accountable for…" actions repeat by area;
- there are few dependencies because no reviewed dependency exists.
**Corrective action:** group area-ownership actions into one action with a list; review dependency semantics (D-2).

### 13. Owner correctness
**Standard:** the right accountable executive and practical owner.
**Evidence:** owners come from the method, consolidated by function. The accountable executive is derived by rule (CFO for finance, procurement, payroll and operations; otherwise the chief executive). P4 states that roles may combine in a small firm.
**Scores:** live E 6.5, C 7.0 · prototype E 7.5, C 7.5 · **improved E 8.5, C 8.6**.
**Deficiencies:**
- the derivation rule is generic;
- "fraud risk lead" and "investigations lead" may not exist in many organisations;
- the assessment does not collect who holds which role.
**Corrective action:** capture role holders in the interview profile (a later method change); until then, say once that roles are functions.

### 14. Timing realism
**Standard:** plausible windows; no invented deadlines.
**Evidence:** every window is labelled "proposed". Each report states that a control works only after a full operating cycle, which may fall after ninety days.
**Scores:** live E 6.5, C 7.0 · prototype E 7.5, C 7.5 · **improved E 8.7, C 8.7**.
**Deficiencies:** stage assignment is rule-based (settle at 30 days, gaps at 60), not sized to the work.
