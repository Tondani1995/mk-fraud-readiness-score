## Fraud pathways (approved structures)

Selection rule: Findings with an approved scenario structure, one per theme where possible; up to 4.

### PATHWAY-01: Invoices are paid for goods that were never delivered or services never performed (from FINDING-03)

- Actor: A supplier inflating its billing, sometimes with the help of an employee who approves the invoices.
- Opportunity: Invoices are paid on approval, without a consistent check that what is invoiced was ordered and received.
- Entry point: An invoice for a delivery, a service or a rate that differs from what was agreed or received.
- Mechanism: The invoice is approved by someone who did not receive the goods or confirm the service, and it is paid in the normal run.
- Weakness relied on: Receipt is not recorded before payment for every invoice, and service delivery is not confirmed by the person who received it.
- Concealment: The invoice carries an approval, so it looks properly processed; overbilling is hidden among ordinary invoices.
- Consequence: Continuing overpayment, which grows where an employee shares in it, and spend that cannot be reconciled to what the organisation actually received.
- Warning indicators: Invoices without a matching receipt or delivery note; Rates above the agreed contract; Service invoices approved by people far from the work; One approver for a supplier's invoices over a long period
- Containment: Hold payment on the suspect invoices, reconcile them to orders and receipts, and preserve the approval trail.
- Long-term response: Every invoice is matched to an approved order and a recorded receipt or confirmed service before payment.

### PATHWAY-02: A deepfake instruction, synthetic identity or generated document defeats a control that relies on recognition (from FINDING-01)

- Actor: An outsider using AI tools to imitate a known person, create a synthetic identity or generate a convincing document.
- Opportunity: Several controls rely on recognising a voice, a face, a signature or a document, and AI can now imitate each of them cheaply.
- Entry point: An instruction, application or document presented through the organisation's ordinary channels.
- Mechanism: The control does what it was designed to do, but the thing it recognises is fake, so a payment, account or approval is granted on false grounds.
- Weakness relied on: The fraud risk assessment has not asked, for each high-risk process, how AI could be used against the controls in it, so the controls have not been tested or redesigned for this.
- Concealment: The control appears to have operated correctly, so the failure is not visible in its records.
- Consequence: Losses through controls that management believes are effective, and a fraud risk assessment that understates exposure in exactly the processes that matter.
- Warning indicators: Requests that rely only on recognising a person or document; Documents with flawless formatting from new counterparties; Video or voice contact that avoids interaction; Identity details that fit together too neatly
- Containment: Confirm the instruction or document with its source through a separate known channel, and suspend reliance on recognition-only checks in the process concerned.
- Long-term response: The fraud risk assessment asks, for each high-risk process, how AI could be used against its controls, and controls that rely on recognition are tested and redesigned.

### PATHWAY-03: Split orders and duplicate invoices pass because nobody looks across the payments (from FINDING-07)

- Actor: A buyer or finance clerk acting alone, or a supplier who deliberately invoices twice.
- Opportunity: Each order and each invoice is checked on its own, so a pattern that only shows across many transactions is never seen.
- Entry point: A purchase split into several orders that each fall under the approval limit, or an invoice resubmitted with a changed number or date.
- Mechanism: Each order is approved within its limit and each invoice passes its own checks, so a purchase that should have gone to tender, or an invoice already paid, is processed in the ordinary way.
- Weakness relied on: No one independent of processing reviews the supplier payments as a population, for splitting, duplicates, unfamiliar suppliers or changed bank details.
- Concealment: Every individual transaction is within policy; only the pattern is wrong, and the pattern is in data that nobody examines.
- Consequence: Overpayment, purchases that bypass competitive tender, and a continuing loss that grows with each payment run until a supplier, an auditor or a whistle-blower raises it.
- Warning indicators: Several orders to one supplier just below an approval limit; Invoices with the same amount and supplier close together; A supplier paid more often than its contract suggests; Orders raised and received by the same person
- Containment: Stop further payments on the suspect orders or invoices, recover any duplicate payment from the supplier, and preserve the order, approval and payment records.
- Long-term response: A senior manager who does not process payments reviews each month's supplier payments for splitting, duplicates, unfamiliar suppliers and changed bank details, and follows up each query.

### PATHWAY-04: An insider who never hands over their work conceals a fraud for years (from FINDING-04)

- Actor: A trusted employee in a sensitive role, such as payroll, creditors or stock, who has worked unchallenged for a long time.
- Opportunity: The role is held by one person who never takes extended leave and whose work is never done by anyone else.
- Entry point: The employee's ordinary, daily processing in their own area.
- Mechanism: The employee manipulates the records they control, and keeps the scheme alive by being present for every transaction and every query that could expose it.
- Weakness relied on: People in sensitive roles are not required to take block leave during which someone else does their work, and the roles that need it are not identified.
- Concealment: The fraud needs continuous attention, which the employee gives; with nobody else ever doing the work, the anomalies are never seen.
- Consequence: A long-running loss that is usually discovered by chance and is large by the time it is found.
- Warning indicators: An employee in a sensitive role who does not take leave; Work that only one person understands or is allowed to do; Queries that are always routed to the same person; Resistance to rotation or process change
- Containment: Place the work with someone else immediately, review the period's transactions, and preserve the records before the employee is approached.
- Long-term response: Sensitive roles are identified and recorded, and everyone in them takes block leave each year during which someone else does their work.

## Decisions

### DECISION-01: Have the answers that cannot both be right settled, and the questions the answers leave open answered, before the controls concerned are relied on.

- Why: 1 scenario has answers that cannot both be right and 5 scenarios raise questions that the answers do not settle.
- Owner: Chief executive; when: Before the controls concerned are relied on or reported as in place.; if delayed: The organisation may rely on a control whose operation the answers do not establish.
- Option "Process owners confirm and correct": Each process owner establishes how the process actually runs, records the answer and corrects the assessment where it was wrong. Effort: Low: a few hours per process owner. Benefit: Fast, and keeps ownership with the people who run the processes. Trade-off: Relies on the same people whose answers are in question.
- Option "Internal walkthrough by someone independent of the process": A manager from another area, or internal audit where it exists, walks through each process with its owner and records what was seen. Effort: Moderate: a day or two per process. Benefit: An answer that does not rest only on the owner's view. Trade-off: Takes longer and draws on people outside the process.
- Option "Independent validation through MK Advisory": MK examines the records and walks through the processes concerned, as a separately scoped engagement. Effort: Higher: a scoped engagement. Benefit: An independent view of whether the controls operate, which the automated assessment cannot give. Trade-off: Cost and time; worthwhile where the answers concern controls the organisation relies on heavily.
- MK recommended route: Process owners confirm and correct, with an independent walkthrough for any answer that is still disputed. Rationale: The answers concern how the organisation's own processes run, which its owners can establish quickly; an independent view is needed only where that does not settle the question.

### DECISION-02: Name one accountable executive for each area with open or unresolved scenarios: fraud risk assessment (1), ethics (1), procurement (1), people (1) and assets (1).

- Why: Each scenario needs one person with authority over the process, the system and the people involved.
- Owner: Chief executive; when: Before design work starts.; if delayed: Open scenarios have no owner, and the controls that stop them stay unassigned.
- Option "One accountable executive per area": Each area with open scenarios has one executive accountable for its controls, as the method suggests. Effort: Low to decide; ongoing for each executive. Benefit: Clear ownership that matches how the organisation is run. Trade-off: Requirements that span areas, such as how changes to payment details are verified, need coordination across owners.
- Option "One executive for the whole programme": A single executive owns every open scenario and directs area leads. Effort: Concentrated on one person. Benefit: One point of accountability and a consistent approach. Trade-off: Area executives may treat the work as someone else's.
- Option "Area owners with a programme coordinator": Area executives own their scenarios, and the fraud risk lead coordinates requirements that span areas and reports progress. Effort: Moderate. Benefit: Ownership stays in the areas while cross-area requirements are handled once. Trade-off: Needs a coordinator with enough standing to hold owners to account.
- MK recommended route: Area owners with a programme coordinator. Rationale: The open scenarios sit in the operating areas, so ownership belongs there; several of the requirements span areas and are best designed once.

### DECISION-03: Decide the order in which the open scenarios are addressed, using explicit factors.

- Why: MK has not approved a method for rating fraud risks, so this report does not rank them, and neither its order nor the method's tiers is a ranking.
- Owner: Chief executive; when: Before design work starts.; if delayed: Effort is spread across every scenario at once, or follows the order of this report, which is not a ranking.
- Option "Explicit factors, settled facts first": Answers to settle come first; then each open scenario is weighed on how far its control is reported in place, whether money, pay, cash or stock can leave without it, how much of the activity exists, and what management already knows of incidents. Effort: Moderate: a structured management session. Benefit: A defensible order that management can explain to the oversight body. Trade-off: Depends on information management must supply, such as volumes and known incidents.
- Option "Area by area": One area is completed before the next begins. Effort: Low to plan. Benefit: Simple to manage and report. Trade-off: Requirements that span areas are designed several times, and exposure in later areas waits.
- Option "Cross-area requirements first": Requirements that span areas, such as verifying changes to payment details, are designed once and applied everywhere, before area-specific controls. Effort: Moderate. Benefit: One design closes several scenarios at once. Trade-off: Area-specific exposures wait until the shared designs are complete.
- MK recommended route: Explicit factors, settled facts first, applying cross-area requirements once where they arise. Rationale: An order of work should rest on facts management can state and defend to its oversight body, and designing shared requirements once avoids building the same control several times.

### DECISION-04: Have the board receive the status of the open and unresolved scenarios at each of its meetings until each is closed, deferred with a reason or accepted.

- Why: Regular reporting keeps the scenarios visible to the people who can remove blockers.
- Owner: Chief executive; when: From the first meeting after this report.; if delayed: Progress is not visible until the next assessment.
- Option "At each meeting of the oversight body": A one-page status per open scenario at each meeting until it is closed, deferred with a reason or accepted. Effort: Low. Benefit: Keeps the scenarios visible to the people who can remove blockers. Trade-off: Adds to the oversight body's agenda.
- Option "Quarterly summary": A consolidated summary each quarter. Effort: Low. Benefit: Less frequent demand on the oversight body. Trade-off: Blockers may wait a quarter before they are seen.
- Option "Exceptions only": Only scenarios that are late or blocked are reported. Effort: Lowest. Benefit: Focuses attention on problems. Trade-off: The oversight body does not see progress, and a scenario can drift without being reported as an exception.
- MK recommended route: At each meeting of the oversight body, until each open scenario is closed, deferred with a reason or accepted. Rationale: Regular reporting is what keeps a programme moving once the first controls are in place.
