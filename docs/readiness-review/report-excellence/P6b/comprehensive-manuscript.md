# The group reports a mature control environment, and its next task is to make that position one the board can rely on

On its own answers, the group has the core controls in place for [[N-REPORTED-HELD]] of the [[N-APPLY]] fraud scenarios that apply to it, and a control reported as a gap in only [[N-GAP|w]]. Every core control is reported in place in fraud governance, in finance and banking, in customers and revenue, and in digital and identity fraud. Taken at face value, that is the profile of an organisation whose fraud programme has moved beyond policy into operation, with ownership, assessment, controls and response all described as working.
<!-- refs: N-REPORTED-HELD, N-APPLY, N-GAP, L-AREAS-HELD -->

MK's reading of the answers is more cautious than the headline, for a reason the answers themselves supply. When a core control is reported in place, the method asks about the practices that make it work: the record it relies on, the list that defines its reach, the independence of the person who carries it out. In [[N-QUALIFIED]] scenarios those practices are reported weaker than the core control. In [[N-CLARIFY|w]] more, the answers leave open how the control can work as described, and in [[N-CONTRA|w]] the answers about the same fact cannot both be true. The organisation has, in other words, described its controls more confidently than it has described the conditions they depend on.
<!-- refs: N-QUALIFIED, N-CLARIFY, N-CONTRA, THEME-ANSWERS -->

This is a common and recoverable position. It usually arises when the core questions are answered by someone who knows the policy and the detailed questions by people who run the process, and it is resolved by asking the process owners how each control actually operates. It nevertheless matters, because fraud is rarely stopped by the existence of a control. It is stopped by a control that reaches every case, compares against something independent and is carried out by someone with no interest in the outcome, and those are exactly the conditions the weaker answers concern.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS, FINDING-03, FINDING-05 -->

[[N-THEMES|W]] themes carry the diagnosis. The first is the gap between the answers and the practices behind them, which runs across every area. The second is that the fraud risk assessment, the process that should direct the controls, is where the answers are least consistent: it reports covering AI-enabled fraud while also reporting that it does not ask how AI could be used against high-risk processes, and it reports deciding a response to every risk above tolerance without a stated way of identifying those risks. The third is that no independent review examines procurement or payroll, which leaves the organisation dependent on the people who run those processes to describe them accurately.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS, THEME-DOUBT-D2, THEME-X-11, FINDING-01, FINDING-02 -->

The themes reinforce one another. An assessment that has not examined how controls could be defeated will not find the gap between a control's description and its operation, and without independent review nothing else will. The result is a position that may well be accurate, but that the organisation cannot yet demonstrate to its board, its auditors or itself.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS, THEME-DOUBT-D2, THEME-X-11 -->

The fraud exposure that follows is specific. If the open answers resolve the weaker way, the group could pay invoices for goods it did not receive, lose stock that its counts would not reveal, allow an insider in a sensitive role to conceal a scheme, and act on an AI-generated instruction through a control that appears to work. None of these is a finding that the event has happened. Each is a route that the answers, as they stand, do not close.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01, PATHWAY-02, PATHWAY-04, FINDING-05 -->

Leadership's priorities follow from this. In the first thirty days, management should settle the AI answers and obtain written answers to the [[N-CLARIFY|w]] open questions from the process owners. In the same period it should name the accountable executives and decide how procurement and payroll will be examined independently. Over the following two months it should put in place the [[N-PUT|w]] controls reported as gaps, together with monitoring of split orders, so that the pattern-level weaknesses in procurement are visible. By the end of the year each control should have met its success measure over a full cycle, and the board should be receiving a status report built on settled answers rather than on the original assessment.
<!-- refs: PRIORITY-01, PRIORITY-02, DECISION-02, N-CLARIFY, N-PUT, PHASE-STABILISE, PHASE-ESTABLISH, PHASE-MATURE -->

The rest of this report sets out what the answers establish, what the organisation should build and how it should get there.

## The controls are described more fully than the practices that make them work

In [[N-QUALIFIED]] scenarios the core control is reported in place while a related practice is weaker, and in [[N-CLARIFY|w]] more the answers raise a question they do not settle. The weaker practices are not peripheral. They include the record a count is compared with, the list that defines who must take block leave, the confirmation of receipt that an invoice is matched against, and the independence of whoever investigates a concern. Each is a condition the core control needs in order to work.
<!-- refs: N-QUALIFIED, N-CLARIFY, FINDING-03, FINDING-04, FINDING-05, FINDING-06 -->

The pattern is consistent with answers given at two levels: the core questions by someone who knows what the policy requires, and the detailed questions by people closer to the process. That reading is an interpretation, but it fits every instance, and it points to a straightforward remedy. The process owners who gave the more cautious answers can say how each control runs, and their written answers will either confirm the position or correct it.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS, PRIORITY-02 -->

The implication for leadership is that the position should not yet be reported to the board as settled. Doing so would turn an unexamined description into a representation the board might rely on.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS -->

## The fraud risk assessment is least consistent exactly where it should direct the rest

The assessment reports covering AI-enabled schemes for every relevant process while also reporting that it does not ask how AI could be used against high-risk processes. It reports deciding a response for every risk above tolerance while reporting that risks are not rated for likelihood and harm. The area's other doubts are related practices reported weaker behind controls reported in place.
<!-- refs: FINDING-01, FINDING-02, AREA-D2-DOUBT -->

The fraud risk assessment is the organisation's instrument for deciding which scenarios need the strongest controls and for revising those controls as threats change. If it has not considered how AI could defeat controls that rely on recognising a voice, a face or a document, it will not have directed attention to the controls most exposed to that change. If it has no consistent way of identifying the risks that exceed tolerance, the decisions it records may follow familiarity rather than exposure.
<!-- refs: THEME-DOUBT-D2, PATHWAY-02 -->

Settling these two answers is therefore not a clerical correction. It determines whether the assessment can serve as the basis for the programme this report proposes, and it should be the fraud risk lead's first task.
<!-- refs: PRIORITY-01, PRIORITY-02 -->

## No independent review examines procurement or payroll

Independent review of procurement and independent review of payroll are both reported weaker than in place, across [[THEME-X-11-AREAS]]. These are the processes where most of the operational questions arise: how receipt is confirmed before invoices are paid, and how block leave reaches every sensitive role.
<!-- refs: THEME-X-11-N, THEME-X-11-AREAS, FINDING-03, FINDING-04 -->

Independent review is the control that ordinarily catches the gap between how a process is described and how it runs. Its absence is what allows the first theme to persist, and the second theme means the risk assessment is unlikely to flag it.
<!-- refs: THEME-X-11, THEME-ANSWERS, THEME-DOUBT-D2 -->

The themes therefore form a closed loop: the answers are stronger than the practices, the assessment that should notice is itself uncertain, and nothing independent looks. Breaking the loop requires action on all three, and the programme in this report is sequenced to do that, settling the answers first, deciding on independent review in the same period and rebuilding the assessment's coverage of AI-enabled fraud.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS, THEME-DOUBT-D2, THEME-X-11, PHASE-STABILISE -->

## The assessment cannot both cover AI-enabled fraud everywhere and leave high-risk processes unexamined

One answer reports that AI-enabled schemes, including deepfake instructions, synthetic identities and generated documents, are assessed for every relevant process; another reports that the assessment does not ask how AI could be used against high-risk processes. High-risk processes are among the relevant ones, so the two cannot both be true.
<!-- refs: FINDING-01 -->

The related answers suggest which way the conflict may resolve. Testing controls against AI-generated fakes is reported as not in place, and including the organisation's own AI tools in scope and simulating an AI attack are reported as partly in place. If the assessment had examined AI-enabled attack on the high-risk processes, those practices would normally follow. The control-system consequence is significant, because several controls elsewhere rely on recognition: call-backs that rely on a known voice, onboarding checks that rely on documents, approvals that rely on a familiar instruction.
<!-- refs: FINDING-01, PATHWAY-02 -->

The target design matters because a control redesigned for AI-enabled attack verifies through a channel the attacker does not control rather than through recognition. Until the assessment has asked the question process by process, management cannot know which of its controls need that redesign.
<!-- refs: FINDING-01, PRIORITY-01 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-01]]

## Responses to risks above tolerance are reported without a stated way of identifying those risks

A response is reported as decided for every risk above tolerance, while likelihood and harm ratings are reported as not in place. Risks above tolerance can be identified without ratings, through a tolerance statement or a documented judgement, so the answers may be consistent; the question is how it is done.
<!-- refs: FINDING-02 -->

The answer shapes everything that follows from the assessment. Where the identification of risks above tolerance is informal, the risks that receive a decided response tend to be the familiar ones, and controls, audit work and training are directed accordingly. The weaker related practices elsewhere in the area point the same way.
<!-- refs: FINDING-02, THEME-DOUBT-D2 -->

The fraud risk lead should document how risks above tolerance are identified today, so that management can decide whether that method is sufficient or whether the assessment needs a more consistent basis before it directs the programme.
<!-- refs: FINDING-02, PRIORITY-02 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-02]]

## The invoice match may not rest on an independent record of what was received

Every invoice is reported as checked against an approved order and a confirmation of receipt before payment, while recording receipt of goods before payment is reported as partly in place and confirmation by the person who received a service as not in place.
<!-- refs: FINDING-03 -->

A three-way match is only as strong as its third element. Where receipt is not written down, the match must rely on another confirmation, and if that confirmation comes from the person who requested the purchase, or from someone far from the work, it is not independent of the invoice. The answers do not say which applies. Approving orders before commitment is also reported as partly in place, which weakens the first element of the match as well.
<!-- refs: FINDING-03 -->

This is the route by which overbilling and fictitious delivery are paid, and it interacts with the procurement-review gap: a supplier's inflated billing would pass each invoice check and would not be seen in aggregate either. The target is a match against a recorded receipt or a confirmed service, made by someone who did not approve the order.
<!-- refs: FINDING-03, FINDING-07, PATHWAY-01 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-03]]

## Block leave is reported for every sensitive role, but no maintained list defines the roles

Block leave is reported as required for everyone in a sensitive role, while identifying high-risk roles on a maintained list is reported as partly in place. The requirement can reach every sensitive role without a list, so the answers need not conflict, but management has no way of showing that it does.
<!-- refs: FINDING-04 -->

Block leave works by forcing someone else to do the work for long enough to expose a scheme that needs continuous attention. It fails quietly when it misses a role, and the roles most likely to be missed are those that have grown informally around one person. Separating conflicting duties during cover is reported as set out but not operating, so even where leave is taken, the person covering may hold duties that let the scheme continue.
<!-- refs: FINDING-04, PATHWAY-04 -->

The HR and payroll lead should establish how the requirement's reach is confirmed and, unless it can be shown another way, maintain the list of sensitive roles that block leave and cover arrangements depend on.
<!-- refs: FINDING-04, PRIORITY-02 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-04]]

## Independent blind counts cannot reveal a loss without an expected quantity to compare with

Counts are reported as independent and blind to system quantities, while stock is reported as not formally recorded. A count reveals a loss only against an expected quantity, and the answers do not say what each count is compared with.
<!-- refs: FINDING-05 -->

The related practices reported weaker are those that create and protect the expected quantity: restricting access to stores, issuing stock only against approved requests, separating custody from the records and explaining differences before adjusting. Together they suggest that the count may be well conducted and still unable to show a loss, because nothing reliable says what should be there.
<!-- refs: FINDING-05 -->

This finding connects with the gap in loss tracking. Losses that are not tracked by location cannot be compared with what counts reveal, so a pattern of shrinkage at one site would not stand out. The finance lead should establish what the counts are compared with before they are described to the board as a control over stock loss.
<!-- refs: FINDING-05, PRIORITY-06, BLUEPRINT-04 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-05]]

## Investigations are reported as independent, but line management or HR conducts them

Every investigation is reported as independent of the area and people concerned, and suspected fraud as investigated by line management or HR. Both can be true if HR, or a manager from another area, investigates; the answers do not say whether a line manager ever investigates their own area.
<!-- refs: FINDING-06 -->

The question matters for the whole response capability. An investigation conducted by someone close to the matter may not withstand a disciplinary hearing or a court, and concerns raised about a manager may be closed by that manager. The exposure is legal as well as practical: where section 34 of PRECCA applies, the matter must be reported to the police, and the criminal case that follows will test how the facts were gathered. The investigations lead should state whether line managers investigate their own areas and, where they do, how independence is secured.
<!-- refs: FINDING-06, PRIORITY-02, LAW-01 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-06]]

## Supplier payments are reviewed only in some areas, so patterns across them can pass unseen

A senior review of supplier payments is reported in place only in some areas, cases or periods, monitoring of split orders is set out but not operating, reviewing payment data for unusual patterns is not in place, and independent review of procurement is partly in place.
<!-- refs: FINDING-07 -->

These four answers describe the same capability from different angles: looking at payments as a population rather than one at a time. Without it, purchases split to avoid tender, duplicate invoices and payments to unfamiliar suppliers each pass their individual checks. It is the clearest instance of the independent-review theme and one of the [[N-GAP|w]] controls reported as gaps.
<!-- refs: FINDING-07, THEME-X-11, N-GAP -->

The target design combines a monthly senior review across every area with monitoring of split orders, each carried out by someone who does not process payments, so that the patterns individual checks cannot see become visible.
<!-- refs: FINDING-07, BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-05 -->

# If the open answers resolve the weaker way, [[N-PATHWAYS|w]] routes to loss remain open

The findings combine. Where an answer proves weaker than reported, one weakness can provide the opportunity and another delay its discovery. The pathways below illustrate how that could happen here. They are plausible sequences built from the answers and MK's scenario library, conditional on the answers resolving the weaker way, and they do not suggest that any event has occurred.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01, PATHWAY-02, PATHWAY-03, PATHWAY-04 -->

## A supplier invoices for more than it delivered, and the approval makes each payment look correct

A long-standing supplier begins to invoice for short deliveries, for partly performed services or at rates above those agreed, sometimes with an employee who approves its invoices sharing in the difference. The opportunity is an invoice check that rests on an approval rather than an independent record of receipt.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01, FINDING-03 -->

Each invoice is approved by someone who did not receive the goods or confirm the service and is paid in the normal run. Because it carries an approval, it looks properly processed, and because supplier payments are not reviewed as a whole in every area, nobody sees that the supplier's billing has drifted. The loss continues until a budget holder questions the spend or the relationship changes, by which time it may be difficult to quantify.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01, FINDING-07 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-01]]

## A generated document or a cloned voice passes a control designed to recognise the real thing

An outsider uses readily available tools to imitate a senior person's voice, generate a convincing document or construct a synthetic identity, and presents it through the organisation's ordinary channels.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-02 -->

The control does what it was designed to do, but what it recognises is fake, so a payment, account or approval is granted on false grounds. If the risk assessment has not asked how AI could be used against each high-risk process, the controls concerned have not been tested or redesigned for this, and their records will show nothing wrong. The loss arises in exactly the controls management believes are effective.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-02, FINDING-01 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-02]]

## A purchase is split below the approval limit, and nobody looks across the orders

A buyer who wants to avoid a tender splits a purchase into orders that each fall below the approval limit, or a supplier resubmits an invoice already paid with a changed number or date. Every transaction is within policy and passes its own checks.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-03 -->

The pattern exists only in the payment data, and without a monthly review in every area and working monitoring of split orders, nobody examines it. Purchases are made at uncompetitive prices, duplicate payments go unrecovered, and the loss grows with each payment run until a supplier, an auditor or a whistle-blower raises it.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-03, FINDING-07 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-03]]

## An insider who never hands over their work sustains a scheme for years

A trusted employee in a sensitive role, such as creditors, payroll or stock, manipulates the records they control and keeps the scheme alive by being present for every transaction and every query that could expose it.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-04, FINDING-04 -->

If the role was never identified as sensitive, block leave does not reach it; if cover is arranged with conflicting duties, the person covering cannot see the scheme either. The fraud needs continuous attention, which the employee gives, so it is usually discovered by chance and is large by the time it is found.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-04, FINDING-04 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-04]]

# The target environment closes the reported gaps and gives management a way to see patterns, not only transactions

The controls that close the [[N-PUT|w]] reported gaps, together with monitoring of split orders, form the core of the target environment. Each blueprint below explains the current position, how the control should operate and how management will know that it works; the specification panel summarises its operating design. The complete specification for every control the register acts on, [[N-CONTROLS]] in all, including implementation activities, is in the supporting workbook. The controls behind the unresolved answers are deliberately not redesigned here, because their design depends on the answers.
<!-- refs: N-PUT, N-CONTROLS, BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-05 -->

## A monthly senior review of supplier payments in every area

The review is reported in place only in some areas, cases or periods. In the target state, a senior manager who does not process payments reviews each month's supplier payments in every area for unfamiliar suppliers, unusual amounts, duplicates and changed bank details, and follows up each query to a recorded answer.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-01 -->

The review is the organisation's principal detective control over procurement, and it connects with the monitoring of split orders and with independent review of procurement: the review finds the anomaly, the monitoring finds the pattern, and independent review confirms that both run. Its first cycle should cover the previous twelve months, so that anything already in the data is followed up.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-05, THEME-X-11 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-01]]

## Link checks before any donation or sponsorship is committed

Checking recipients for links to officials, customers' decision-makers and employees is reported in place only in some cases. In the target state, every donation or sponsorship passes the check before money is committed, any link is disclosed and approved, and the approval file shows both.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-02 -->

The control is simple and preventive. Its value lies in reaching every commitment, which is why its measure is a complete approval file for each one, and why a commitment found without the check triggers a review of everything paid before it was found. It also carries legal weight: section 34A of PRECCA makes the organisation liable when someone associated with it pays a bribe unless it had adequate procedures, and whether procedures were adequate is for a court to decide on the record the organisation can produce.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-02, LAW-02 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-02]]

## Supervisor approval of hours and overtime before payment

Approval of hours by the employee's supervisor is reported in place only in some areas. In the target state, every timesheet is approved by the supervisor against the roster or attendance record before it is paid, and the system prevents the person who processes a timesheet from approving it.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-03 -->

The design places the check with the person who knows whether the work was done, and enforces segregation in the system rather than relying on instruction. A timesheet approved by the person who processed it is re-approved independently, and that person's other timesheets for the period are reviewed.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-03 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-03]]

## Losses tracked by location and reported each month

Tracking losses of cash, stock and assets is reported in place only in some areas. In the target state, losses are tracked over time and by location and reported to management each month with the actions taken.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-04 -->

Loss tracking is the control that turns individual write-offs into a pattern. It also supports the stock finding: once losses are tracked by location, they can be compared with what the counts reveal, and a site where shrinkage concentrates becomes visible.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-04, FINDING-05 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-04]]

## Monitoring of split orders that actually runs

Monitoring of split orders is reported as set out but not yet operating. In the target state, orders to each supplier are reviewed each month for several smaller orders that together exceed an approval limit, by someone independent of the buyers, and each instance is followed up to a recorded conclusion.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-05 -->

Because the control is already designed, the work is to make it operate: to produce the report, name the reviewer and run the first review over the last twelve months. It complements the senior review of supplier payments, which looks at payments, by looking at the orders behind them.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-05, BLUEPRINT-01 -->

<!-- section: COMP-OPMODEL -->
# Ownership already sits in the right places, and the operating model gives it a rhythm and an independent check

The organisation reports that executive accountability for fraud risk is written into a named executive's role and that the board holds final oversight. The target operating model builds on that. The chief executive remains accountable overall; the fraud risk lead coordinates the assessment, challenges the control owners and consolidates reporting; the owners of procurement, finance, payroll, ethics and investigations operate the controls in their processes and keep the records; and the investigations lead receives reports and alerts and investigates independently.
<!-- refs: ROLE-OVERSIGHT, ROLE-EXECUTIVE, ROLE-RISK, ROLE-OWNERS, ROLE-DETECT -->

[[exhibit:operating-model]]

What the model adds is a rhythm and an independent check. Status on each open scenario moves upward each month from the control owners to the fraud risk lead and on to the chief executive, and to the board at each meeting. Escalations follow the same route. The independent check is the decision the themes call for: how procurement and payroll will be examined by someone outside those processes, whether a manager from another area, internal audit or an external engagement.
<!-- refs: ROLE-RISK, ROLE-OVERSIGHT, THEME-X-11, DECISION-04 -->

The management information follows from the position. Until every answer is settled, each monthly report should open with the answers to settle and the questions to clarify, and with the corrections they produced. The controls come next: each measure missed or escalated in the period and, once the independent examination of procurement and payroll is running, what it found. The board's version should be brief enough to read at every meeting and specific enough to show whether the position it relies on is the one the records support.

# Over twelve months the programme moves from settled answers to controls that have proven themselves over a full cycle

The programme runs in four proposed stages. The windows are proposals for management's own plan, not deadlines, contractual dates or estimates of how long any control takes to design or to show that it works. Answers are read against MK's method of fraud scenarios and controls. <!-- section: COMP-CONCLUSION -->
# The group may already have the controls it reports, and a year from now it should be able to show it

The group's answers describe a mature fraud-control environment, and they may be right. What the assessment shows is that the organisation cannot yet demonstrate it: several controls are described more fully than the practices they depend on, the fraud risk assessment is uncertain on AI-enabled fraud and on how risks above tolerance are identified, and nothing independent examines procurement or payroll.
<!-- refs: THEME-ANSWERS, THEME-DOUBT-D2, THEME-X-11 -->

The transition required is from a described position to a demonstrated one. The foundations are settled answers, an independent check on the processes where the doubts concentrate, and a risk assessment that asks how controls could be defeated. On those foundations the [[N-PUT|w]] controls reported as gaps, and the monitoring of split orders, close the routes the answers leave open.
<!-- refs: PRIORITY-01, PRIORITY-02, BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-05, DECISION-01 -->

By ninety days, every open answer should be settled, every open area owned, the approach to independent review decided and the priority controls running their first cycles. By twelve months, each control should have met its measure over a full cycle, the assessment should have been refreshed on the corrected answers, and the board should be receiving a status report it can rely on.

MK Advisory would add value at two points: where an answer remains disputed after the process owners have responded, and once the programme is running, to examine whether the controls the organisation relies on most operate as designed. Both call for the evidence and walkthroughs that this assessment, by its nature, does not include.
