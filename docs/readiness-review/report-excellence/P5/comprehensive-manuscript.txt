# The group's framework stops short of its operations, and the first step is to establish what its own functions know

(Executive diagnosis)

On its own answers, the group has the core controls in place for 46 of the 106 fraud scenarios that apply to it, a core control reported as a gap in 58, and core answers outstanding for two more. In fraud governance, finance and banking, and detection and response, every core control is reported in place. In third parties, people and payroll, customers, assets and stock, and digital channels, almost every core control is reported as a gap.

MK's reading is that the group has built the central parts of a fraud programme and has not yet carried them into the operations. Ownership is assigned, finance and banking controls are described as working, and concerns can be reported and investigated. The controls that would prevent and detect fraud inside the processes, where suppliers and staff are paid, customers receive credit and refunds, stock is held and instructions arrive, are largely absent.

What distinguishes this assessment is the information behind it. Seven questions put to the functions that run these processes came back as "not known", from five functions. The risk function could not say whether process owners take part in the risk assessment or whether it is updated when the business changes; payroll could not say whether its data is analysed; finance could not say whether customer payments are matched to invoices; and sales could not say where refunds are paid. Nothing is concluded from these answers, but they show that in several places the people closest to a control cannot describe it.

Four themes carry the diagnosis: the unknowns inside the functions, the framework that has not reached the operations, a verification weakness that recurs across four areas, and the absence of independent review over procurement and payroll. They interact. Where a function does not know how its control works, it cannot notice when the control fails; where requests are accepted on the requester's word, the failure is easy to induce; and where nobody looks across procurement or payroll, a resulting loss would be found by a supplier, a customer or a whistle-blower rather than by the group.

The exposure is concrete. A supplier's bank details can be changed and confirmed through the fraudster's own number, credit can be granted on documents checked only on appearance, refunds below a threshold can be raised and approved by the same person, and an employee in a sensitive role that was never identified may never hand over their work.

Leadership's priorities follow. In the first thirty days, every outstanding question should be assigned to a named person and answered or recorded as an open item, every operating area should have an accountable executive, and the order of work should be decided on explicit factors. Over the following two months, the priority controls should be designed and put to work: supplier bank changes confirmed through details on file, a call-back that holds whatever a caller appears to be, the monthly senior review of supplier payments, verification of credit applications with their source, and block leave for every sensitive role. By twelve months, each should have met its measure over a full cycle and the board should be receiving status on every area and every open question.

# This blueprint interprets the group's own answers and designs the response; it does not verify that controls operate

(Scope and analytical basis)

The analysis rests on answers given by the group's respondent and the functions to which questions were referred, read against MK's fraud scenarios and controls. It covers 106 fraud scenarios across ten areas. Answers given as "not known" are reported as outstanding and are never treated as weaknesses or as controls in place. MK has not examined documents, interviewed staff or tested any control, and where the report names a record, it is a record management should keep.

The blueprint's value lies in what it does with the answers: it reads them together, draws them into themes, traces how the weaknesses could combine into fraud, and designs the controls, operating model, decisions and programme that would close them. Independent examination of whether controls operate belongs to MK Advisory, which is scoped and priced separately.

# The centre holds, the operating areas are open, and the unknowns sit in both

(Readiness profile)

In three areas every core control is reported in place: fraud governance and accountability, finance, banking and financial reporting and detection, speak-up and response. In five areas nearly every core control is reported as a gap: third parties and supply chain, people, payroll and insider risk, customers, revenue and products, assets, cash and inventory and digital, cyber-enabled and identity fraud. The fraud risk assessment would sit with the central areas but for questions its own function could not answer.

The open areas hold most of the scenarios, with 14 gaps in digital channels, 13 in third parties and 11 in people and payroll. The outstanding answers fall in risk assessment, payroll, customers, assets and the speak-up process, so the unknowns are not confined to the open areas; they also limit how far the central framework can be relied on.

# Four themes explain the position, and the first determines how far the others can be addressed

(Systemic themes)

The themes below draw the answers into a management view. The map shows where each appears, and the discussion explains how they interact.

## Several functions do not hold answers about their own controls

The 7 questions answered "not known" concern ordinary, operational facts: whether process owners take part in the risk assessment, whether it is refreshed when the business changes, whether payroll data is analysed, whether customer payments are matched to invoices, where refunds are paid, whether the asset register is checked physically and whether the risk of retaliation against a reporter is assessed.

Each is treated as outstanding. Together they suggest that, in several processes, fraud controls are understood at the centre rather than by the people who run the process, which is consistent with the second theme. The practical consequence is that the group cannot yet rely on these controls, and it cannot design their replacements without knowing what already exists.

## The framework at the centre has not been carried into the operating processes

Governance, finance and detection are reported as complete, and the five operating areas as almost entirely open. The framework establishes who owns fraud risk and how concerns are handled; it does not stop a supplier being paid into the wrong account or stock leaving a store. Those outcomes depend on controls inside processes the centre does not run.

The response is organisational first: the executives who run procurement, payroll, customers, stock and digital channels should own the controls in their processes, with the centre setting the standard and checking that it is met.

## The same verification weakness recurs across suppliers, payroll, customers and digital channels

Across third parties and supply chain, people, payroll and insider risk, customers, revenue and products and digital, cyber-enabled and identity fraud, 13 practices that would verify a request through a route the requester does not control are reported weaker than in place. Supplier bank changes are confirmed through details in the request, a familiar voice is accepted, customer and employee changes are not consistently verified, and credit documents are checked on appearance.

Most payment diversion and impersonation succeeds by supplying the confirmation a process asks for. Because the weakness is shared, so is the remedy: verification through details already held, by a channel the requester did not choose, designed once and applied everywhere.

## No independent review examines procurement or payroll

Independent review of procurement and of payroll are reported weaker than in place across third parties and supply chain and people, payroll and insider risk. The senior review of supplier payments is agreed but not working, the payroll review looks only at totals, and payroll could not say whether its data is analysed.

Independent review is what would reveal both the controls that do not operate and the functions that do not know. In its absence, the themes reinforce one another, and the programme in this report addresses them together: answers first, ownership next, a shared verification standard, and independent review of the processes that move the most money.

# Seven findings show how the themes appear in the processes that move money, goods and credit

(Findings)

The findings are the scenarios through which each theme appears most clearly: within each theme, the scenario with more core controls reported absent, or with the most related practices also reported weaker. They follow the method's order of areas, which is not a ranking; The full register is in the supporting workbook.

## Supplier bank changes are confirmed through details the fraudster can supply

Changes to supplier bank details are confirmed, but sometimes through contact details taken from the request, and a follow-up call from someone who sounds like the usual contact is accepted.

Both describe a confirmation the fraudster controls. The change feeds the supplier master file from which every later payment is made, and the monthly review that would look for changed bank details does not yet run, so one accepted change can divert every payment until the supplier complains.

The target separates the confirmation from the request: details on file, a call-back that holds whatever the caller appears to be, and a second person who did not capture the change.

## The senior review of supplier payments has been agreed but does not run

The monthly senior review is set out but not working, split orders are not yet monitored in practice, payment data is not reviewed for unusual patterns, and independent review of procurement is only partly in place.

These describe one missing capability, looking at payments as a population. It is the detective control behind the preventive ones, catching a changed bank detail that slipped through, a duplicate invoice or a split purchase. Because the design exists, the target is operational: a named reviewer outside payments processing, a monthly cycle and a first review over the last twelve months.

## Credit is granted on documents checked only when something looks wrong

Documents are verified only if something looks wrong, and independent approval of credit, bureau checks and the investigation of early defaults are each only partly in place. Finance could not say whether customer payments are matched to invoices.

A check triggered by appearance is the one generated payslips and statements pass. With early defaults not consistently investigated and payment matching unknown, application fraud would surface as bad debt and could repeat with the same templates.

The target verifies the key information in every application with its source before approval, and finance should establish whether payments are matched to invoices.

## Block leave reaches most sensitive roles, but the roles are not fully identified

Most people in sensitive roles take block leave with someone else doing their work. High-risk roles are only partly identified, avoiding conflicting duties during cover and avoiding single points of knowledge are set out but not operating, and there is no fair route for concerns or support for staff under financial pressure.

Block leave fails quietly when it misses a role, and the roles most likely to be missed are those that have grown informally around one person. Cover by someone with conflicting duties can let a scheme continue even during the leave.

The target identifies the sensitive roles, keeps the list current and requires block leave in every one, with cover by someone without conflicting duties.

## Customer refund and delivery details can be changed by sales staff

Most changes to customer details are verified, but sales staff can still make them. Defined onboarding is not in place, business customers are only partly verified, and details shared between accounts are not flagged.

The ability to change customer details is the ability to redirect refunds and goods. Moving the change out of sales and verifying it with the customer through details on file restores the separation that deters collusion. A changed record that lets an impostor see or use a customer's personal information is also a security compromise under POPIA, which section 22 requires the group to report to the Information Regulator and the customer. The response is among the first and is specified in full in the workbook.

## Payroll is reviewed at the level of totals, and nobody could say whether its data is analysed

Someone other than the preparer looks at each run, but only at the totals; headcount verification is not in place, independent review is only partly in place, and payroll could not say whether its data is analysed.

There is no point at which the payroll is compared with the people the group actually employs, so a ghost employee or a leaver left on the payroll would not stand out. The target is a detailed review of each run before payment by someone who did not prepare it, and payroll should establish whether its data is analysed.

## Refunds are approved independently only when they are large or staff are unsure

Independent approval of refunds and credit notes is sought only for large refunds or when staff are unsure. Returns are not received before credit, refund patterns and reversals are only partly reviewed, and sales could not say whether refunds go back to the original source.

A refund raised and approved by the same person below the approval threshold is one of the oldest ways to steal from a retailer, and if refunds can be paid somewhere other than the original source, the money leaves without a customer ever being involved. The target is independent approval of every refund and a rule that refunds return to the original source.

# The open controls create four practised routes to loss, each hard to see from inside the process

(Fraud exposure and pathways)

The pathways that follow trace how the open controls could be used together: one weakness opens the route and another keeps the loss from being noticed. Each is a plausible sequence assembled from the organisation's answers and MK's scenario library, written to show management where to intervene, and none implies that the event has happened.

## An employee who is never away sustains a scheme for years

A trusted employee in a sensitive role manipulates the records they control. Because the role was never identified as sensitive, block leave does not reach it, and nobody else does the work.

The scheme needs continuous attention, which the employee gives. Where leave is taken, cover by someone with conflicting duties may not see it either, and the fraud is usually found by chance, by which time it is large.

## A supplier's bank details are changed and the next payment run pays the fraudster

An outsider who has compromised a supplier's email requests a change of bank details with a contact number in the request; finance calls it and the fraudster confirms. Sometimes a follow-up call in the usual contact's voice is enough.

The next genuine invoice is paid to the new account. Every document is genuine, and with no monthly review looking for changed bank details, nothing looks wrong until the real supplier asks why it has not been paid.

## A purchase is split below the approval limit, and nobody looks across the orders

A buyer splits a purchase into orders under the approval limit, or a supplier resubmits an invoice already paid. Each transaction passes its own checks, and the pattern exists only in data nobody examines. The loss grows with each payment run until a supplier, an auditor or a whistle-blower raises it.

## Fabricated payslips secure credit that is never repaid

An applicant presents a generated payslip and bank statement that look genuine, so the appearance-based check is never triggered. Credit is approved, goods are taken and the account defaults.

Because early defaults are not consistently investigated, the loss is booked as bad debt, and the same templates can be used again before anyone recognises the pattern.

# The target environment verifies every change through a route the requester does not control and closes the routes insiders use

(Target fraud-control environment)

The blueprints below cover the priority controls. They share two principles: a change that redirects money, goods or credit is confirmed through details the group already holds, and no one in a sensitive role controls a process alone for long enough to conceal a fraud. The panel summarises each control's operating design; the complete specification for every control in the register, 103 in all, is in the supporting workbook.

## Supplier bank changes confirmed through details already on file

Changes are confirmed today, but sometimes through details in the request. In the target state, every change is confirmed with the supplier through contact details already on file before it takes effect, by someone other than the person who captured it, and logged with how it was confirmed.

The control works with the call-back rule and with the monthly payment review, which looks for changed bank details among its patterns.

## A call-back rule that holds whatever the caller appears to be

A follow-up call from the supplier is accepted today. In the target state, a call back on the number on file is required for every change request, whatever an incoming call or video appears to show, and staff are briefed and tested.

Designed once for supplier changes, the same rule can protect employee bank changes, customer details and instructions from executives.

## A monthly senior review of supplier payments that actually runs

The review is agreed but not working. In the target state, a senior manager who does not process payments reviews each month's supplier payments for unfamiliar suppliers, unusual amounts, duplicates and changed bank details, and follows up each query to a recorded answer, starting with a look back over the previous twelve months.

## Credit applications verified with their source before approval

Documents are checked today only on appearance. In the target state, the key information in every credit application is verified with its source before approval, and the file records what was verified and how. The control connects with independent approval of credit, bureau checks and the investigation of early defaults.

## Block leave for every sensitive role, with independent cover

Most people in sensitive roles take block leave today. In the target state, everyone in an identified sensitive role takes a block of consecutive leave each year, with their work done by someone without conflicting duties, and exceptions are approved and recorded.

The control depends on the list of sensitive roles, so maintaining that list is part of its design, and it connects with avoiding single points of knowledge and a fair route for concerns.

# Ownership must move into the operating areas, and every function must know the controls it owns

(Target fraud-risk operating model)

The group reports that executive accountability for fraud risk is written into a named role and that the board holds final oversight. The target operating model keeps both and adds owners for the controls inside the operations. The chief executive remains accountable overall; the fraud risk lead sets standards, challenges the owners and consolidates reporting; the owners of finance, payroll, customers and the other operating areas run the controls and keep the records; and the investigations lead handles reports and alerts independently.

For this group the model carries a specific expectation: each control owner should be able to say how their controls operate, which is what the outstanding questions show is not yet the case. Status moves upward each month from the owners through the fraud risk lead to the chief executive and to the board at each meeting, including the questions that remain open.

The management information should keep the unknowns visible until they close. Each month the fraud risk lead should report to the chief executive the outstanding questions answered and still open, by the function that holds them, the stage reached by every open scenario, and each control measure missed or escalated. The board should see the same picture at a higher level: which functions still owe answers, which priority controls have completed a full operating cycle, and which escalations needed an executive decision. A report that shows the unknowns shrinking is itself evidence that ownership has moved into the operations.

# Four decisions rest with leadership, and resolving the unknowns comes first

(Leadership decisions)

The decisions below are those only executives can take. For each, the options and trade-offs are set out with MK's recommended route; the choice remains management's.

## How the outstanding questions will be resolved

MK recommends assigning each of the 7 questions to a named person and recording any that cannot be answered as an open item with an owner, because an answer or an owned open item is what allows the scenarios they affect to be decided.

## Who will be accountable for each open area

Six areas have open scenarios. MK recommends that each operating executive owns the scenarios in their area, with the fraud risk lead coordinating the requirements that span areas.

## The order in which the open scenarios will be addressed

The order should rest on facts management can defend. MK recommends resolving the unknowns first, then weighing each open scenario on explicit factors, and applying the shared verification requirement once wherever it arises.

## How the board will see progress

MK recommends a one-page status for each open area at every board meeting, covering both the controls and the questions that remain open, until each is closed, deferred with a reason or accepted.

# Over twelve months the programme replaces unknowns with answers and carries the framework into the operations

(Twelve-month implementation blueprint)

The programme runs in four proposed stages. The windows are proposals for management's own plan, not deadlines, contractual dates or estimates of how long any control takes to design or to prove. A control counts as working only once it has met its success measure over a full operating cycle.

Stabilise resolves the outstanding questions or gives each an owner, names the accountable executives, records the order of work and approves the designs, beginning with the shared verification standard. Establish puts the priority controls in place and runs their first cycles.

Embed extends the work to the remaining open scenarios in the order management has decided, informed by the answers obtained in the first stage, and strengthens the related practices. Mature measures each control over a full cycle, arranges the independent examination of procurement and payroll, refreshes the scenarios and reports to the board. Every action is in the supporting workbook.

# The scorecard shows whether the controls work and whether the unknowns are closing

(Management fraud-readiness scorecard)

Each control measure describes what its records should show once it operates; the programme measures track the response itself. None is reported as met today.

For this group the first measure to watch is the number of outstanding questions answered by the function that holds them, because it shows whether the operating functions have taken ownership. The second is whether the priority controls have met their measures over a full cycle.

# The position rests on the organisation's own answers, read together against MK's method

(Methodology and limitations)

Answers are read against MK's method of fraud scenarios and controls, and together as well as one at a time. Answers given as "not known" or declined are reported as outstanding and never as weaknesses.

The executive diagnosis states the position in four parts: the calculated result, the answers that are inconsistent, the reading of the answers and MK's conclusion. Material findings, blueprints and stages are chosen and sequenced by the rules stated where each appears, not by a ranking of fraud risk.

# Within a year the group should know how its controls work and have carried its framework into the operations

(Management conclusion)

The group should recognise two things. Its fraud programme is complete at the centre and largely absent in the operations, and several of the functions that run those operations cannot yet say how some of their own controls work. The first is a gap in controls; the second is a gap in knowledge, and it has to be closed first.

The transition required starts with answers and ownership: every outstanding question resolved or owned, and every operating area with an accountable executive. On that foundation, a single verification standard should protect every change that redirects money, goods or credit, the monthly review of supplier payments should run, sensitive roles should be identified and covered by block leave, and someone independent should look across procurement and payroll.

By ninety days, the unknowns should be resolved or owned, the owners named and the priority controls running their first cycles. By twelve months, those controls should have met their measures over a full cycle, the remaining scenarios should be in progress in the order management decided, and the board should be receiving status it can rely on.

MK Advisory would add value once the first controls are running, to examine whether they operate as designed and to support the independent review of procurement and payroll, which call for the records and walkthroughs that this assessment does not include.
