# The firm's financial and ethical controls are sound, and its fraud defences now need to reach the channels through which instructions arrive

On its own answers, the firm has the core controls in place for [[N-REPORTED-HELD]] of the [[N-APPLY]] fraud scenarios that apply to a firm of its size and activity, and a core control reported as a gap in [[N-GAP]]. A further [[N-NOT-EXPECTED|w]] scenarios are not expected of a firm this size and are not counted against it. Every gap falls in one area, digital, cyber-enabled and identity fraud, where none of the [[AREA-D9-APPLY]] scenarios that apply has its core control in place.
<!-- refs: N-REPORTED-HELD, N-APPLY, N-GAP, N-NOT-EXPECTED, AREA-D9-GAP, AREA-D9-APPLY -->

MK's reading is that the firm has built the fraud controls a business of its size most needs and has not yet extended them to its communications and systems. Ownership of fraud risk is written into a named role, the risks are assessed, ethical standards are set, finance and banking controls are described as working, and there is a way to report and respond to concerns. Those controls govern what happens once an instruction reaches the firm's processes. What the answers report as absent are the controls that decide whether an instruction is genuine in the first place.
<!-- refs: THEME-HELD, THEME-AREA, L-AREAS-HELD -->

That distinction matters because modern fraud against small firms rarely attacks the finance controls directly. It arrives as an email from a mailbox that has been taken over, an instruction in a cloned voice, a request to the help desk from someone claiming to be a colleague, or a sign-in to the banking platform with a stolen password. Each of these supplies the instruction that the firm's sound controls then carry out.
<!-- refs: THEME-AREA, THEME-VERIFY, PATHWAY-01, PATHWAY-02 -->

[[N-THEMES|W]] themes carry the diagnosis: a sound core framework, an exposure concentrated in digital and identity fraud, and a habit, across those channels, of accepting a request on the requester's word. They interact simply. The core framework is in place, but it depends on instructions being genuine, and the digital controls that would establish that are missing.
<!-- refs: THEME-HELD, THEME-AREA, THEME-VERIFY -->

The response is unusually tractable. Most of what is needed is a set of settings and one rule: multi-factor sign-in on every mailbox and every system that moves money; confirmation through a separate, known channel for every payment instruction received by email or call; verified help desk resets; and a set procedure for notifying the Information Regulator and the people affected if personal information is compromised, as POPIA requires. Within ninety days, these can be in place and tested; within twelve months, each should have met its measure over a full cycle and the owners should be receiving a short status report at each meeting.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-02, BLUEPRINT-03, BLUEPRINT-04, BLUEPRINT-05, PRIORITY-06, PHASE-ESTABLISH, PHASE-MATURE -->

Every question in the assessment was answered, so the position is not limited by missing information.

# [[N-THEMES|W]] themes explain the position, and the second and third are the same weakness seen from two sides

The map shows where each theme appears; the discussion explains what each means and how they connect.
<!-- refs: THEME-HELD, THEME-AREA, THEME-VERIFY -->

[[exhibit:theme-map]]

## The core fraud framework is in place for a firm of this size

Ownership, risk assessment, ethics, finance and banking, and detection and response are all reported complete. For a firm with fewer than fifty people that is a strong foundation, and it means the response does not need to build a fraud programme from the beginning.
<!-- refs: THEME-HELD, L-AREAS-HELD -->

The foundation has one dependency the answers make visible: it assumes that the instructions it acts on are genuine. Payment approvals, segregation and reconciliations work on the instruction they are given, so their strength depends on controls in the digital area that the answers report as absent.
<!-- refs: THEME-HELD, THEME-AREA -->

## The exposure is concentrated in email, calls, sign-in and personal information

All [[AREA-D9-GAP]] gaps sit in digital and identity fraud. Email and the systems that move money are protected by passwords alone, payment instructions received by email are acted on as received, calls and voice notes are verified at discretion, help desk resets are usually but not always verified, access to personal information is limited for some systems only, and a compromise would be handled without a set notification process.
<!-- refs: AREA-D9-GAP, FINDING-04, FINDING-06, FINDING-03, FINDING-01, FINDING-05, FINDING-07, FINDING-02 -->

These gaps divide naturally into two groups: controls that stop an outsider getting in, such as multi-factor sign-in and verified resets, and controls that stop an instruction being believed, such as the email rule and call verification. A firm needs both, because a taken-over mailbox defeats the second group unless the first is in place, and a spoofed email defeats the first unless the second is.
<!-- refs: THEME-AREA, THEME-VERIFY -->

## Requests are accepted on the requester's word

Within the digital area, [[THEME-VERIFY-N]] practices that would verify a request through a route the requester does not control are reported weaker than in place: email instructions, calls and voice notes, changes to call-back details and help desk resets.
<!-- refs: THEME-VERIFY-N, FINDING-03, FINDING-01, FINDING-05 -->

The defence in every case is the same rule: confirm through details already held, by a channel the requester did not choose, whoever the requester appears to be. In a small firm where everyone knows the owners' voices, the rule has to bind the owners as well, because their authority is what an impersonator borrows.
<!-- refs: THEME-VERIFY, BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-03 -->

<!-- section: COMP-FINDINGS -->
# [[N-FINDINGS|W]] findings show where an outsider could reach the firm's money, its clients or its information

The findings are the scenarios through which each theme appears most clearly. They follow the method's order, which is not a ranking; The full register of [[N-FINDINGS-REGISTER]] findings is in the supporting workbook.
<!-- refs: N-FINDINGS, N-FINDINGS-REGISTER -->

## Instructions by phone, voice note or video depend on individual judgement

Whether an unusual instruction received by phone, voice note or video is checked depends on the judgement of the person who receives it. Three supporting practices are partial: treating pressure as a reason to verify, verifying changes to call-back details, and refusing any request for gift cards or PINs.
<!-- refs: FINDING-01 -->

The owners' voices are known to everyone, which makes them easy to trust and easy to imitate from a short recording. Because the firm's payment controls act on instructions, an instruction believed on the strength of a voice passes through them. The target is one agreed verification method that binds everyone, including the owners.
<!-- refs: FINDING-01, BLUEPRINT-01 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-01]]

## A compromise of personal information would be handled without a set process

A compromise would be handled as it happens, without a set process, and warning the people affected is not in place.
<!-- refs: FINDING-02 -->

POPIA requires notification of the Information Regulator and the people affected as soon as reasonably possible. Without a procedure, the decision would be made under pressure, and the people affected could not protect themselves against the fraud that often follows a compromise. The target is a written procedure, tested once a year.
<!-- refs: FINDING-02, BLUEPRINT-02 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-02]]

## Payment instructions received by email are acted on as received

Payment changes received by email are acted on as received, with no rule requiring confirmation through a separate channel.
<!-- refs: FINDING-03 -->

This is the point at which business email compromise succeeds, whether the email is spoofed or sent from a genuine mailbox that has been taken over. Because the email rule and multi-factor sign-in protect against different halves of the same attack, the firm needs both. The target is a rule that no payment, salary or bank detail received by email is acted on without separate confirmation.
<!-- refs: FINDING-03, FINDING-04, BLUEPRINT-03 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-03]]

## Email accounts are protected by passwords only

Every email account, including the owners' and shared mailboxes, is protected by a password alone.
<!-- refs: FINDING-04 -->

A password obtained by phishing or reused from another website gives an outsider the mailbox itself, with its correspondence and its authority. From there, the outsider can intercept invoices to clients, send instructions to staff and reset other accounts. The target is multi-factor sign-in on every account with no exceptions, confirmed each month.
<!-- refs: FINDING-04, PATHWAY-01, BLUEPRINT-04 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-04]]

## Help desk resets are usually, but not always, verified

Most password and multi-factor resets need verification through a trusted route, such as a manager or a number on file.
<!-- refs: FINDING-05 -->

A reset is how an impersonator converts a convincing story into access, and an attacker looks for the exception. Once multi-factor sign-in is in place, the reset becomes the main route around it, which is why the reset and the second factor belong together. The target is verification of every reset through a route the caller does not control.
<!-- refs: FINDING-05, PATHWAY-04, BLUEPRINT-05 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-05]]

## The systems that move money can be entered with a password

Remote access, cloud systems and the banking platform are protected by passwords only.
<!-- refs: FINDING-06 -->

A stolen password for the banking platform would let an outsider change beneficiaries or release payments in the firm's name, bypassing the approvals the firm relies on. Multi-factor sign-in on every system that can move money or change master data closes this route; it is among the first responses and is specified in the workbook.
<!-- refs: FINDING-06, PRIORITY-06 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-06]]

## Access to personal information is limited for some systems only

Access to personal information is limited to those who need it for some systems only.
<!-- refs: FINDING-07 -->

The wider the access, the larger the loss if one account is compromised, and the more convincingly an outsider can impersonate the firm's clients. Limiting access in every system and restricting bulk exports reduces both the value of a compromised account and the harm a compromise causes.
<!-- refs: FINDING-07 -->

[[exhibit:finding:FINDING-07]]

<!-- section: COMP-PATHWAYS -->
# The open controls create [[N-PATHWAYS|w]] routes by which an outsider could take money from the firm or its clients

In each pathway, one weakness gives an outsider access and another lets the instruction through. They are plausible sequences built from the answers and MK's scenario library; they do not suggest that any of these events has occurred.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01, PATHWAY-02, PATHWAY-03, PATHWAY-04 -->

## A mailbox is taken over and the firm's own invoices divert its income

An outsider obtains a staff member's email password and, with no second factor, signs in. They watch the firm's correspondence with clients and, at the right moment, send an invoice with changed bank details from the firm's real address.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01, FINDING-04 -->

Clients pay the outsider, the firm notices only when expected payments do not arrive, and its relationships with those clients are damaged. Forwarding rules set by the outsider may hide the clients' replies from the mailbox owner.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-01 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-01]]

## A cloned voice of an owner instructs an urgent payment

An outsider imitates an owner's voice from a short public recording and calls, or sends a voice note, asking for an urgent and confidential payment. The check depends on whoever takes the call, and under urgency and rank the payment is made. It appears properly authorised until the owner is asked.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-02, FINDING-01 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-02]]

## A payment instruction received by email is acted on without separate confirmation

An email that appears to come from a supplier or an owner asks for an urgent payment or a change of bank details. Because email instructions are acted on as received, it is processed in the ordinary way, and recovery depends on how quickly the bank is alerted once the supposed sender is asked.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-03, FINDING-03 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-03]]

## A help desk reset hands an outsider the account that moves money

An outsider calls whoever handles the firm's IT, claims to be a colleague with a lost phone, and asks for a reset. If this is one of the resets that is not verified, the outsider signs in as the colleague and, with no second factor on the banking platform, changes beneficiaries or releases a payment under a real account.
<!-- refs: PATHWAY-04, FINDING-05, FINDING-06 -->

[[exhibit:pathway:PATHWAY-04]]

<!-- section: COMP-TARGET -->
# The target environment establishes that every instruction is genuine before the firm's sound controls act on it

The blueprints below cover the priority controls. Together they protect the point at which instructions enter the firm: who can sign in, how a reset is verified, and how a request made by email, call or voice note is confirmed. The complete specification for every control in the register, [[N-CONTROLS]] in all, is in the supporting workbook, including multi-factor sign-in on the systems that move money.
<!-- refs: N-CONTROLS, PRIORITY-06 -->

## One verification method for calls, voice notes and video, binding everyone

Calls and voice notes are checked today at individual discretion. In the target state, an agreed method, such as a call-back on a known number or a code word, verifies every request made by phone, voice note or video, it applies to everyone including the owners, and staff know they are expected to use it.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-01 -->

The control connects with treating pressure as a trigger, verifying changes to call-back details and banning requests for gift cards or PINs. Its measure is a briefing for everyone and a test in which the method was followed in every case.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-01 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-01]]

## A notification procedure for compromises of personal information

A compromise would be handled today without a set process. In the target state, a procedure sets out who decides, who notifies the Information Regulator and the people affected, and how quickly, as section 22 of POPIA requires, and it is tested once a year.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-02 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-02]]

## A rule that no email instruction is acted on without separate confirmation

Email instructions are acted on as received today. In the target state, every payment, salary or urgent request received by email is confirmed through a separate, known channel before it is acted on, and the rule is briefed and tested.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-03 -->

The rule and the call verification method are best introduced together, as one standard for confirming instructions, so that staff learn a single habit.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-03, BLUEPRINT-01 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-03]]

## Multi-factor sign-in on every email account

Every mailbox is protected by a password alone today. In the target state, multi-factor authentication is required for every email account, including the owners' and shared mailboxes, with no exceptions, and coverage is confirmed each month.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-04 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-04]]

## Every help desk reset verified through a route the caller does not control

Most resets are verified today. In the target state, every password or multi-factor reset is verified through a route the caller does not control, such as a call-back to a number on file, and each reset is logged with how it was verified.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-05 -->

[[exhibit:blueprint:BLUEPRINT-05]]

# Over twelve months the programme closes the digital routes and confirms that the controls stay in force

The programme runs in four proposed stages. The windows are proposals for the firm's own plan, not deadlines, contractual dates or estimates. A control counts as working only once it has met its success measure over a full operating cycle.
<!-- refs: PHASE-STABILISE, PHASE-ESTABLISH, PHASE-EMBED, PHASE-MATURE -->

Stabilise names the accountable person and records the order of work. Establish switches on multi-factor sign-in, introduces the confirmation rule for email and calls, verifies resets and writes the notification procedure. Embed strengthens the related practices, such as treating pressure as a trigger and limiting access to personal information. Mature confirms each month that no mailbox or system has been added without a second factor, tests the rules and the procedure, and reports to the owners at each meeting.

<!-- section: COMP-CONCLUSION -->
# The firm has sound controls for a business of its size, and within a year its digital defences should match them

The firm should recognise that its fraud controls are sound wherever it has applied them. Its exposure lies in the channels through which instructions reach those controls, and it is concentrated enough to close with a focused programme.
<!-- refs: THEME-HELD, THEME-AREA -->

The transition required is to establish, before acting on any instruction, that it is genuine: multi-factor sign-in on every mailbox and every system that moves money, verified resets, and one rule for confirming instructions by email, call or voice note that binds everyone. A notification procedure completes the set.
<!-- refs: BLUEPRINT-01, BLUEPRINT-03, BLUEPRINT-04, BLUEPRINT-05, BLUEPRINT-02 -->

By ninety days, these controls should be switched on or written, briefed and tested. By twelve months, each should have met its measure over a full cycle, and the owners should be confirming at each meeting that coverage has not slipped.
<!-- refs: PHASE-ESTABLISH, PHASE-MATURE -->

MK Advisory would add value if the owners want an independent check that the controls are in force across every mailbox and system, or support in testing the verification rule and the notification procedure.
