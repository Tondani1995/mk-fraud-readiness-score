# Reporting-quality correction: MK's direction of 12 November 2026

> **Revised on 12 November (second direction)** by `REPORT-REVISION.md`: relationship rules 0.2, four position statements instead of the R-1 threshold, no ranking language, the control-content library, proposed planning stages and candidate sufficiency rules 0.2. The reports below are kept in `report-correction/` as the "before" of `REVISION-COMPARISON.md`.

Continued from local commit `5fb4dc2` on `review/mk-integrated-readiness`. This is a targeted correction of the prototype's report content, not a further architecture exercise. Every organisation is fictional, every calculated figure is a labelled test fixture, the revised method stays inactive, and no historical MFRS score, product, price, order, entitlement, payment or fulfilment path, shared preview or deployment was touched. Nothing has been pushed or merged. The AI manuscript stage and a real customer workflow remain untested, so none of these reports is a certified paid report, and each says so on every page.

## Outcome

The corrected reports make fewer claims, and the claims they make can be traced and defended. Of the 21 controls the first prototype told the overstated organisation were "not supported in practice", the question wording shows 2 genuine contradictions and 4 controls that rely on a weaker practice; the other 15 now read as separate, weaker defences, with the reported control left standing. All 790 ratings, periods, difficulty labels and deadlines the first prototype derived from tier or answer class are withheld, and the reports say why. Standards are now worded in each question's own format: no report says "How is stock recorded? The answer is yes", where previously 405 such sentences and 450 question-prompt concatenations reached the four sets of reports. The Comprehensive management sections are shorter (13 or 14 pages against 17 or 18 for the retail organisations) and read as analysis rather than count tables waiting for an AI stage, while every register is complete in the appendices and the supporting workbook, reconciled row by row. What the reports still cannot do, because MK has approved no method for it, is say which open scheme matters most; they say that plainly and leave the order to management, and a decision-ready proposal for that method is separate.

## Change register

| Area | First prototype | Corrected |
|---|---|---|
| Contradictions | Any weaker practice of a scheme made its reported control "false comfort", "not supported in practice" | Pair by pair; only answers that cannot both be right, or a method-established dependency, leave a control unresolved |
| Engine cross-check label | "Conflicting answer" whenever the cross-checked practice was not in place | "Supporting practice not in place: whether this contradicts the confirmation depends on how the two questions relate"; outcome code kept |
| Risk ratings | Priority from tier, likelihood from answer class, impact from tier | None; "Not rated", with the reason |
| Timing and difficulty | 30, 60 or 90 days per finding, decision deadlines, difficulty from gap count | Six phases ordered by information, decision, prerequisites, build, strengthen, operate; no dates |
| Tier | "First-priority fraud schemes" | Tier 1, explained as the stage at which the method confirms a scheme, not a rating |
| Standards | "The answer to '<question>' is yes, across everything it should cover" | Level 4 for anchored questions, the in-place option in its own words for named options, the in-place answer for practice questions |
| Measures | "Every case passes this test: <question>" | One success measure per control, from its standard |
| Impact and cycle | Generic statements from the prototype's own tables | The method's own description of why a scheme matters, or its linked risks; a cycle only where the method states one |
| Controls | One blueprint per finding with up to six controls run together | One row per control, whatever number of schemes it serves, referred to elsewhere by reference |
| Roles | Up to 46 method owner labels | 5 to 13 consolidated roles, with the method's wording behind each in Appendix I |
| Cover | The calculated figure as the readiness score | R-1 convention; "Calculated figure (test fixture)" wherever it appears |

| Edge cases | A confirmation established from a wider answer was treated as unanswered; one not asked at the organisation's size as not analysable | Counted as in place through the wider answer; reported as not expected at this size, outside the sufficiency shares |

## Page-by-page review

Every page of all eight corrected PDFs was rendered and looked at, the overstated organisation's Comprehensive page by page and the others by section. The review found and fixed, in the same pass: the fixed prototype banner covering section labels and repeated table headers on continuation pages (replaced by an in-flow strip on each section and the label in every page footer); a management-priorities row pushed into the footer; "0 of them in tier 1", "in each of the 1 scheme" and "0 were declined"; "most of them in" two areas when gaps were spread one per area, and "spread across 6 areas" when three held most; area names nesting "and" inside lists; "Interrupted by: Interim step" and a manual check offered for schemes whose answers needed resolving; a scheme's name used as a noun phrase; Section 5 listing 51 further defences control by control; Section 2 repeating Section 3's scheme lists; the same sentence repeated in every row of Appendices A, C and G; customer risk references that read like the method's own risk codes; first-person option labels ("Our own", "we send"); and a Section 9 conclusion spilling one line onto an empty page. Tables break between rows, every register row is printed (RQ-09) and no page clips content.

Remaining limitations, stated plainly: the relationship table covers the 56 pairs the fictional organisations produce out of 826 in the method, and the rest default to complementary weakness; 688 of the method's 821 practice questions have no description beyond the in-place answer and no records, so recommendations for them stay generic by design; the scenario interpretation is fixed-rule text, not the AI manuscript; and nothing has been read by a customer.
