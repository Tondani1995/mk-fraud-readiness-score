Sections 1–9 are the management report. The appendices hold every scheme, risk, control, record, action, measure, answer relationship and outstanding answer behind it, and the supporting workbook holds the same registers with the method references that trace each row to the question asked and the answer given.

Basis of this report

This report is produced by fixed rules from the organisation's answers to the MK Fraud Readiness review, read against the draft review method, which MK has not yet approved. The AI manuscript stage has not been run; the interpretation in each section is fixed-rule text. No evidence has been examined, no control has been tested for operating effectiveness, no one has been interviewed and no assurance opinion is given. Records named in this report are records management should maintain; independent review of evidence is a separate Advisory service.

Section 4 · Fraud scenario portfolio

How the schemes would work here

Plausible pathways for an organisation with the controls it reported, using the method's own description of each scheme. They are not allegations that any event has occurred.

An email account is taken over

S01 · digital channels · tier 1
How it starts
A mailbox without strong sign-in is used to send or intercept payment instructions.
Why it matters
Most business email compromise starts with an account that has no multi-factor authentication.
What the answers show
Multi-factor on all email: email accounts are protected by passwords only.
What would interrupt it
Multi-factor authentication is required for every email account, including executives' and shared mailboxes, with no exceptions.
Warning signs
Takeover of staff accounts; Business email compromise and payment redirection
Links
F01, FR01, C01

A payment instruction received by email is acted on

S02 · digital channels · tier 1
How it starts
Changed details or urgent payments are processed on the strength of an email.
Why it matters
Acting on payment instructions received by email is how business email compromise succeeds.
What the answers show
Email never enough on its own: payment changes received by email are acted on as received.
What would interrupt it
An organisation-wide rule requires confirmation through a separate, known channel for every payment, salary or urgent request received by email, and staff follow it.
Warning signs
Business email compromise and payment redirection
Links
F02, FR02, C02

A cloned voice or video instruction is obeyed

S03 · digital channels · tier 1
How it starts
A caller who sounds or looks like a known person is believed.
Why it matters
Voices and faces can be cloned, so only an agreed verification method holds.
What the answers show
Verification for calls, voice notes and video: some staff check unusual requests, depending on their own judgement. Behind it, pressure triggers verification is partly in place, changes to call-back details verified is partly in place and gift cards and PIN requests banned is partly in place.
What would interrupt it
An agreed method, such as a call-back on a known number or a code word, applies to everyone, including executives, and staff know they are expected to use it.
Warning signs
Deepfake voice and video impersonation of executives and staff
Links
F03, FR03, C03, C04, C06, C05

A security compromise is not notified

S04 · digital channels · tier 1
How it starts
The Regulator and affected people are not told, so they cannot protect themselves.
Why it matters
POPIA section 22 requires notification as soon as reasonably possible.
What the answers show
Compromises reported as POPIA requires: a compromise would be handled as it happens, without a set process. Behind it, affected people warned is not in place.
What would interrupt it
A procedure sets out who decides, who notifies the Information Regulator and the people affected, and how quickly.
Warning signs
Theft and sale of personal information
Links
F04, FR04, C07, C08

A help desk reset hands an attacker access

S05 · digital channels · tier 2
How it starts
An impersonator persuades the help desk to reset a password or authenticator.
Why it matters
The method links it to social engineering of the IT help desk and takeover of staff accounts.
What the answers show
Help desk resets verified: most resets need verification through a trusted route, such as the person's manager or a number on file.
What would interrupt it
Every password or multi-factor reset is verified through a route the caller does not control, such as a call-back to a number on file or a manager's confirmation through a known channel.
Warning signs
Social engineering of the IT help desk; Takeover of staff accounts
Links
F05, FR05, C09
