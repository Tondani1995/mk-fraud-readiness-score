# Classifying answers that appear to conflict

> **Superseded in part on 12 November (second direction).** The rules and the reviewed table are replaced by `ANSWER-RELATIONSHIP-RULES.md` (relationship-review-0.2): unreviewed pairs are no longer assumed complementary, routing no longer establishes a dependency, "not applicable" is no longer an untested practice, and five of the six unresolved pairs below are now reviewed questions to clarify. This document is kept as the record of the first correction.

MK's direction of 12 November 2026, item 1. The first prototype called a confirmation "false comfort" whenever any other practice of the same fraud scheme was reported as a gap, and so reported 21 controls of the overstated organisation as "not supported in practice". That inferred that a control cannot operate merely because an associated practice is weaker, which the method does not establish. The corrected evidence model (`src/lib/reports/revised/relationships.ts`) classifies each pair instead.

## Five relationships

For every control reported fully in place, each practice of the same scheme that is weaker, not yet used or outstanding is compared with it:

| Relationship | When | What the report says |
|---|---|---|
| Direct contradiction | The two answers describe the same fact and cannot both be true as given | The answers cannot both be right; the scheme is not treated as covered until management establishes which is correct |
| Necessary-control dependency | The method's wording or structure establishes that the reported control uses the practice's output (a receipt the match checks, a list of roles the leave rule applies to, ratings the tolerance test compares) | The control can operate only as far as the practice does; it is unresolved until the practice is in place or the answer corrected |
| Complementary weakness | A further defence against the same scheme is weaker, and the method establishes no dependency | The further defence is weaker; the answer given for the control stands and is not described as disproved |
| Untested practice | The organisation reports that no case has arisen, so the practice has not been used | Nothing is concluded either way |
| Missing information | The practice was declined, answered "I don't know" or is still waiting | Nothing is concluded; the question is listed as outstanding |

The order of evidence for a classification: missing information and untested practices are decided by the answer itself; then the reviewed pair table (`relationship-review-v0.1.json`, a rationale for each of the 56 pairs reviewed so far); then the method's structure (a confirmation the method asks only where the practice exists, or a route the practice's answer precludes); and otherwise the default, complementary weakness, which never claims a confirmation is disproved. Only direct contradictions and necessary-control dependencies leave a scheme unresolved, and only they count towards R-1.

The engine's cross-check outcome previously labelled "Conflicting answer" whenever the cross-checked practice was not in place. Its label now reads "Supporting practice not in place: whether this contradicts the confirmation depends on how the two questions relate"; the outcome code is unchanged for contract readers, and the reports classify each cross-check through the same table. Of the overstated organisation's ten cross-checks, one is a direct contradiction (AI-enabled fraud) and nine found a separate practice weaker.

## The 21 controls the first prototype flagged

Recalculated for the overstated organisation (`report-correction/P6b/model.json`, test RQ-01): 2 genuine direct contradictions, 4 necessary-control dependencies (five pairs; three-way match depends on two receiving practices) and 15 complementary weaknesses whose confirmations stand as reported. The other three organisations each had one flagged control (code of conduct explained to staff, against training on joining); it is a complementary weakness in all three, because the confirmation's own question names induction as only one of the places standards are explained.

| Scheme | Control reported in place | Corrected classification | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| An investigation is not independent or fair | Investigators independent | Direct contradiction | The answers about investigations are hard to reconcile: one says every investigation is done by people independent of the area concerned, the other that suspected fraud is investigated by line management or HR. Unless line managers never investigate matters in their own area, both cannot be right. |
| AI-enabled schemes are not considered | AI-enabled fraud covered | Direct contradiction | The answers about AI-enabled fraud cannot both be right as given: one says AI-enabled schemes are assessed for every relevant process, the other that the assessment does not ask, process by process, how AI could be used against it. |
| Risks above tolerance are left without action | Response decided | Necessary-control dependency | Responses are reported for every risk above tolerance, but risks are reported as not rated for likelihood and harm. The method identifies risks above tolerance by comparing ratings with the approved tolerance, so the reported response process has no established way of telling which risks are above it. |
| Invoices are paid for goods or services not received or not agreed | Three-way match | Necessary-control dependency | Three-way match is reported for every invoice, but receipt of goods is reported as recorded before payment for most deliveries only. The match can check an invoice against receipt only where receipt has been recorded, so it cannot operate for every invoice as reported. For services, the person receiving the service is reported as not confirming delivery before payment, which is the receipt the match relies on for service invoices. Service invoices cannot be checked against receipt as reported. |
| An insider in a high-risk role works unchecked | Block leave required | Necessary-control dependency | Block leave is reported for everyone in a sensitive role, with compliance monitored, but high-risk roles are reported as known informally rather than listed. ## What remains for MK

The reviewed table is a prototype classification: 56 pairs, each with its rationale, written from the question wording, the level selected and the method's structure. Pairs not yet reviewed default to complementary weakness. MK's decision is whether to approve the table and the rule, and whether the method should carry these relationships itself (for example a "requires" field on a question), so that classification does not depend on a separately maintained table.
