An insider or outsider adds a supplier that does not exist and invoices it. Fictitious suppliers enter through onboarding that can be skipped. Here, new supplier process is reported in place, but new supplier approved by another person: not in place; directors compared with staff: not in place.

Management does not know how fraud could happen in its own processes. Whether risks are understood decides whether controls are aimed at the right schemes. Here, fraud risks assessed is reported in place, but management has not seen or accepted the results; process owners take part: not in place.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: management formally accepts the results, and every significant risk has a named owner who has agreed to it.

Warning signs: Fraud schemes that are never identified, so no control is designed to stop them · Effort and audit work not directed at the highest fraud risks · Controls that do not match how fraud actually happens
