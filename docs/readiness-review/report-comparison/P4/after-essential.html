How could that exposure materialise?

How the schemes would work here

A payment instruction received by email is acted on

Changed details or urgent payments are processed on the strength of an email
›
Email never enough on its own not in place
›
Business email compromise and payment redirection

Changed details or urgent payments are processed on the strength of an email. Acting on payment instructions received by email is how business email compromise succeeds. Here, payment changes received by email are acted on as received.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: an organisation-wide rule requires confirmation through a separate, known channel for every payment, salary or urgent request received by email, and staff follow it.

Warning signs: Business email compromise and payment redirection · Takeover of staff accounts

›
Multi-factor on all email not in place
›
Takeover of staff accounts

A mailbox without strong sign-in is used to send or intercept payment instructions. Most business email compromise starts with an account that has no multi-factor authentication. Here, email accounts are protected by passwords only.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: multi-factor authentication is required for every email account, including executives' and shared mailboxes, with no exceptions.

Warning signs: Takeover of staff accounts · Business email compromise and payment redirection · Spoofed or lookalike email in the organisation's name

Deepfake voice and video impersonation of executives and staff

A caller who sounds or looks like a known person is believed. Voices and faces can be cloned, so only an agreed verification method holds. Here, some staff check unusual requests, depending on their own judgement; pressure triggers verification: partly.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: an agreed method, such as a call-back on a known number or a code word, applies to everyone, including executives, and staff know they are expected to use it.
