How could that exposure materialise?

How the schemes would work here

Unauthorised commitments and approval override

Overbilling, fictitious deliveries or inflated rates are paid. Invoices paid without checking approval and receipt are how overbilling and fictitious deliveries get paid. Here, invoices are paid without being checked against what was approved or received; quantities checked: not in place.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: every invoice is checked against an approved order, quote or contract and against confirmation of receipt before payment, and differences are resolved first.

Warning signs: Unauthorised commitments and approval override · Duplicate or false invoices · Overpricing, overbilling and inflated variations

Supplier payments are diverted through changed bank details

Supplier payments are diverted through changed bank details
›
Familiar caller still verified not in place; change confirmed with supplier partly
›
Payment redirection through false bank details or impersonation

A fraudster, often impersonating the supplier by email, voice or video, has the bank account changed. Changed supplier bank details are the most common route for payment fraud, and cloned voices now defeat call-backs that rely on recognition. Here, a follow-up call from the 'supplier' is accepted as confirmation; changes are confirmed, but sometimes using contact details taken from the request itself.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: the procedure requires a call back on the number on file regardless of any incoming call or video, and staff know why.

Warning signs: Payment redirection through false bank details or impersonation

Salaries are redirected through a fake bank change

A request that appears to come from an employee changes where their salary is paid
›
Bank changes confirmed with employee not in place
›
Salary redirection through changed bank details

A request that appears to come from an employee changes where their salary is paid. Salary redirection through fake bank change requests is common and fast. Here, bank detail changes are made as requested.

Interrupted by: Until the design operates, the process owner checks every new case and the recent ones by hand against this standard: every change is confirmed through a channel the organisation starts, such as a call to a number on file or in person, before it takes effect.

Warning signs: Salary redirection through changed bank details · Ghost or fictitious employees and payees · Unauthorised pay increases, allowances or deductions
