# The 17 objectives without a scenario confirmation (generated)

> HYPOTHETICAL: research simulation, not an approved or production value. Generated by `scripts/research/objectives/review.mjs` from bank 1.3-review, coverage map 1.3 and review engine build 1.6. The proposed changes are applied to an in-memory copy of the coverage map only. Fictional organisations only. Frame figures are a pooled mean of midpoint values (anchored level x 20; full 90, partial 40, designed 30, not in place 0) with no caps, no treatment for contradictions and no visibility rule, so the three frames can be compared on the same basis; they are not proposed scores.

## Test used

A scored confirmation is proposed only where all four hold: (a) the objective describes a control that on its own prevents or detects a material fraud scenario, not a quality attribute, document or maturity feature of another control; (b) it applies to a broad class of organisations; (c) an organisation could report every existing confirmation in place while this control is absent, so the frame would otherwise overstate readiness; (d) a reporting promise relies on it. Otherwise the objective stays diagnostic (asked in function packages, reported as findings and blueprint current state, never scored), or sits outside the scored frame (facts and case history).

## Why each objective is treated as it is

**OBJ-D2-ownership: Ownership and participation.** Remains diagnostic. Management acceptance and participation in the assessment are the conditions under which FS-D2-05 (a response is decided for risks above tolerance, D2-P-01) is met. The confirmation that matters is the response; ownership explains a gap in it.

**OBJ-D2-identification: How fraud risks are identified.** Confirmation added to an existing scenario. FS-D2-01 ("Fraud risks are never identified") is confirmed by D2-G-01, which records only that an assessment exists. An assessment that names general labels ("theft") identifies no scheme a control can be tested against. Adding D2-I-01 as a second confirmation on the route the scenario already uses for organisations with an assessment mirrors its existing route for informal or no assessment (D2-N-04).

**OBJ-D2-rating: How fraud risks are rated.** Remains diagnostic. Rating method, inherent and residual ratings and comparison with tolerance describe how mature the assessment is. FS-D2-05 already confirms that risks above tolerance receive a decided response.

**OBJ-D2-evidence: Documentation and connections.** Remains diagnostic. Documentation and links to other risk work make the assessment defensible and shared; they are attributes of the assessment, not a control over a fraud scenario. MK may want legal advice on whether documentation should carry more weight for the PRECCA adequate-procedures position.

**OBJ-D3-training: Training and awareness.** Confirmation added to an existing scenario. No confirmation asks whether the people most likely to be targeted (finance, procurement, payroll, sales and IT) are trained on the frauds they face. FS-D3-10 ("Staff do not know or understand what is expected") is confirmed by D3-C-04, which concerns standards of conduct. D3-A-03 is added as its second confirmation. FS-D3-10 is tier 3; whether training of high-risk roles warrants a higher tier is left to MK (prioritisation is an open decision).

**OBJ-D3-monitoring: Monitoring the ethics programme.** Remains diagnostic. Review of the ethics programme and trend reporting apply mainly to larger and public organisations and describe the second and third line over the ethics programme, not a control over a scenario.

**OBJ-D4-scope: How buying is organised.** Outside the scored frame. A scope objective: its questions switch later modules on or off. Its one practice-typed question (D4-S-07, written contracts for ongoing or significant work) is recorded as a data observation: MK may want it retyped as a fact or moved to OBJ-D4-contracts.

**OBJ-D4-orders: Purchase orders and approval.** Confirmation added to an existing scenario. FS-D4-05 ("Invoices are paid for goods or services not received or not agreed") is confirmed by D4-V-01, the check at the point of payment against "what was approved". Where nothing is approved before the commitment, the check compares the invoice with an approval raised after the event. Approval before commitment (D4-P-01, "through a purchase order or another recorded approval") is the "agreed" half of the scenario and applies to every organisation that buys.

**OBJ-D4-receiving: Receiving goods and services.** Remains diagnostic. Confirmation of receipt is already part of the FS-D4-05 confirmation (D4-V-01 asks for a check "against confirmation that the goods or services were received"). D4-R-01 also assumes goods: no fact in bank 1.3 separates organisations that buy only services, so as a confirmation it would be answered as a gap by service-only buyers.

**OBJ-D4-contracts: Contract management.** Remains diagnostic. FS-D4-13 confirms the decisive control (variations approved before work goes ahead, D4-K-04). The register, tracking and reviews describe how well contracts are administered.

**OBJ-D4-monitoring: Monitoring and assurance.** Remains diagnostic. FS-D4-12 confirms monthly senior review of supplier payments (D4-V-15). Data analysis, reviewer independence and independent examination deepen detection; they describe its quality.

**OBJ-D5-highrisk: High-risk roles and privileged access.** Confirmation added to an existing scenario. FS-D5-12 ("An insider in a high-risk role works unchecked") is confirmed by mandatory block leave (D5-K-01). Block leave protects only the roles the organisation has identified as high-risk; without D5-H-01 (high-risk roles identified) the confirmation can be reported in place for an undefined population. It is currently a follow-up; it moves to the confirmations of the same scenario.

**OBJ-D5-conduct: Behavioural and lifestyle indicators.** Remains diagnostic. Behavioural indicators, financial-wellness support and lifestyle reviews are sensitive and partly regulated (POPIA, labour law). They inform recommendations; scoring them would reward intrusive practices.

**OBJ-D5-verification: Payroll verification and monitoring.** Confirmation added to an existing scenario. FS-D5-02 ("Ghost employees are paid") is confirmed by a review of each payroll run (D5-P-01) or of the bureau report (D5-O-05). Those reviews look at changes and totals; a ghost already on the payroll passes them every month. Matching the people paid to real employees (D5-V-01) is the control that finds one. It is currently a follow-up; it moves from the follow-ups to the confirmations of the same scenario.

**OBJ-D8-gateway: Physical exposure.** Outside the scored frame. Establishes which physical things of value the organisation holds. D8-S-04 (how stock is recorded) is a fact that opens the stock controls.

**OBJ-D10-lastcase: The most recent case.** Outside the scored frame. Facts about the most recent case. They show how fraud is found and handled here and feed narrative comparisons; one event is not a standing control and would make the frame depend on whether a case happened.

**OBJ-X-11: Independent assurance examines high-risk processes.** Remains diagnostic. Independent assurance over high-risk processes is third-line activity that tests controls rather than operating one. Many owner-managed organisations have no internal audit and cannot reasonably be expected to. A size-conditioned tier 3 confirmation could be considered, but that conditioning is itself an MK decision.

## The minimal coverage-map change

Five questions already in bank 1.3 change role from related or follow-up to confirmation on the scenario they already belong to. No question text, level description, option, scenario, tier or weight changes, and no new question or scenario is added. Two promoted questions are removed from their scenario's follow-ups so they are not asked twice; the consistency check then uses the remaining follow-ups.

What the numbers show. The five changes move P6b by -2.6 points against -24.5 under A+C, and P6c by -2.6 against -24.5. Most of the A+C movement comes from adding 59 practices that describe how mature, documented or thorough a control is; scoring them would make the number measure the depth of a domain's question set as much as the presence of its key controls, and would penalise organisations in domains where the bank asks more detail. The five promoted controls are the ones whose absence the frame could not see at all: each now counts once as a confirmation. They do not, and are not meant to, reproduce the A+C figure.

P6b and P6c give the same figures on every frame because the frames read only the confirmations and the practices of the 17 objectives, which the two profiles answer identically; they differ only in the supporting practices of confirmed scenarios, which no frame reads. The consistency check is what tells them apart (`staged-assessment/INFORMATION-GAIN-AND-CONSISTENCY.md`, section 5).

The overstatement P6b models (every confirmation reported in place, every supporting practice reported weak) is a contradiction between answers, not a gap in the frame. Build 1.6 surfaces it through the consistency check, which records each contradiction it finds without changing any figure; whether contradictions should limit a score or a band (F2 and F4) stays inactive until MK decides. The respondent pays for the five changes with one extra screen per promoted question that applies.

## What stays open for MK

- Whether these five changes are adopted, and whether any diagnostic objective (in particular OBJ-X-11, independent assurance, for organisations above a size MK sets) should also be confirmed.
- The tier of FS-D3-10 once it carries training of high-risk roles (tier 3 today).
- D4-S-07 is typed as a practice inside a scope objective; MK may want it retyped as a fact or moved to OBJ-D4-contracts.
- Bank and coverage-map changes need a new coverage-map version, a new ruleset hash and a regression run before any session uses them; none of that is done here.
