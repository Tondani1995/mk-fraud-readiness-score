C02 Multi-factor on all email
IT lead · S31
Reported today
Email accounts are protected by passwords only.
Target operating practice
Multi-factor authentication is required for every email account, including executives' and shared mailboxes, with no exceptions.
Implementation activities
  1. List every email account in scope and how it is set today.
  2. Apply the setting to each, starting with executives' and finance mailboxes, and record any approved exception.
  3. Produce the email security report monthly, and follow up any gap.
Records to keep · E02
Email security report
Cycle: monthly
Success measure · M02
At the end of the period, the email security report shows multi-factor authentication on every email account, including executives' and shared mailboxes, and any exception is approved and recorded.
If it fails
Close the gap, check activity for the period it was open, and treat any unexplained activity as a security incident.
C03 Email never enough on its own
Chief financial officer · S32
Reported today
Payment changes received by email are acted on as received.
Target operating practice
An organisation-wide rule requires confirmation through a separate, known channel for every payment, salary or urgent request received by email, and staff follow it.
Implementation activities
  1. Write the rule and have it approved by the chief executive.
  2. Brief staff who act on payment or salary requests, including executives and their assistants, and record who has been briefed.
  3. Test the rule at least once a year, for example with a simulated request, and act on the result.
Records to keep · E03
Verification rule record
Cycle: each year
Success measure · M03
All staff who act on payment or salary requests were briefed by the end of the period, and the most recent test found the rule followed in every case tested.
If it fails
Where the rule was not followed, establish why, brief the people concerned again and check whether any payment or disclosure resulted.
C04 Verification for calls, voice notes and video
Chief financial officer · S33
Reported today
Some staff check unusual requests, depending on their own judgement.
Target operating practice
An agreed method, such as a call-back on a known number or a code word, verifies any request made by phone, voice note or video; it applies to everyone, including executives, and staff know they are expected to use it.
Implementation activities
  1. Write the rule and have it approved by the chief executive.
  2. Brief staff who act on requests by phone, voice note or video, including executives and their assistants, and record who has been briefed.
  3. Test the rule at least once a year, for example with a simulated request, and act on the result.
Records to keep · E04
Verification method record
Cycle: each year
Success measure · M04
All staff who act on requests by phone, voice note or video were briefed by the end of the period, and the most recent test found the rule followed in every case tested, including requests that appeared to come from executives.
If it fails
Where the rule was not followed, establish why, brief the people concerned again and check whether any payment or disclosure resulted.
C08 Compromises reported as POPIA requires
IT lead · S34
Reported today

A procedure sets out who decides, who notifies the Information Regulator through its eServices portal and the people affected, and how quickly, as section 22 of POPIA requires.
Implementation activities
  1. Write the procedure, naming who decides and who acts at each step.
  2. Brief the people with a role in it, and keep the contact details it relies on current.
  3. Test it each year with an exercise or a real case, and record what changed as a result.
Records to keep · E08
Breach response procedure

C10 Help desk resets verified
IT lead · S35
Reported today
Most resets need verification through a trusted route, such as the person's manager or a number on file.
Target operating practice
Every password or multi-factor reset is verified through a route the caller does not control, such as a call-back to a number on file or a manager's confirmation through a known channel.
Implementation activities
  1. Write the confirmation step into the procedure: a call-back to a number on file, or a manager's confirmation through a known channel, never details supplied with the request.
  2. Limit who can make the change in the help desk system, and log how each change was confirmed.
  3. Check the password and multi-factor resets made in the last six months against the log, and confirm any that were not verified.
Records to keep · E10
Reset log
Cycle: whenever a reset is requested
Success measure · M10
Every password or multi-factor reset made in the period was confirmed through a route the caller did not control before it took effect, or is a recorded and approved exception.
If it fails
Suspend any password or multi-factor reset that was not confirmed until it is verified, and check whether any payment, delivery or access used it.
