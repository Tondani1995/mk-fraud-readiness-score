Section 4 · How the scenarios could occur

Illustrative exposure mechanisms

Included: every scenario with answers to settle or a question to clarify (up to three), then the first scenario with a control reported as a gap in each area, in the method's order, up to six in all. They are plausible pathways for an organisation with the controls it reported, not allegations that any event has occurred, and their inclusion is not a ranking.

Donations or sponsorships channel bribes

S26 · ethics
How it starts
A payment to a cause linked to a decision-maker buys influence.
Why it matters
The method links it to donations and sponsorships used as disguised bribes and undeclared conflicts of interest steering decisions.
What the answers show
Recipients checked for links: reported in place only in some areas, cases or periods.
What would interrupt it
Before any donation or sponsorship is committed, the recipient is checked for links to public officials, customers' decision-makers and employees, and any link found is disclosed and approved.
Warning signs
Donations and sponsorships used as disguised bribes; Undeclared conflicts of interest steering decisions
Links
F02, FR01, C02

A fictitious supplier is created and paid

S29 · procurement
How it starts
An insider or outsider adds a supplier that does not exist and invoices it.
Why it matters
Fictitious suppliers enter through onboarding that can be skipped.
What the answers show
New supplier process: checks happen only when someone thinks to do them. Behind it, directors identified is partly in place, directors compared with staff is not in place and suppliers re-checked is set out, not yet operating.
What would interrupt it
Every new supplier passes set checks, including registration, tax number, bank account and directors, and is approved before it can be paid.
Warning signs
Fictitious or shell suppliers
Links
F03, FR02, C03, C04, C05, C06, C07

Salaries are redirected through a fake bank change

S42 · people
How it starts
A request that appears to come from an employee changes where their salary is paid.
Why it matters
Salary redirection through fake bank change requests is common and fast.
What the answers show
Bank changes confirmed with employee: bank detail changes are made as requested.
What would interrupt it
Every change to an employee's bank details is confirmed through a channel the organisation starts, such as a call to a number on file or in person, before it takes effect.
Warning signs
Salary redirection through changed bank details
Links
F15, FR14, C31

Refunds are stolen

S65 · customers
How it starts
One person raises and approves refunds or credit notes to themselves or accomplices.
Why it matters
Refunds processed and approved by one person are a classic way to steal.
What the answers show
Refunds approved independently: approval is sought only for large refunds or when staff are unsure. Behind it, refunds to the original source is not in place, returns received before credit is not in place and refund patterns reviewed is partly in place.
What would interrupt it
Every refund and credit note is approved by someone other than the person who processes it.
Warning signs
False refunds, credit notes and returns
Links
F26, FR25, C51, C52, C53, C54, C55, C56

Cash takings are stolen

S75 · assets
How it starts
Takings are under-recorded or removed before banking.
Why it matters
Daily reconciliation of takings is the main control over cash theft.
What the answers show
Daily reconciliation: takings are reconciled occasionally, or only when there is a shortage.
What would interrupt it
Takings at every cash point are reconciled to sales records daily, and every shortage and surplus is investigated.
Warning signs
Theft of cash; Sales skimmed before they are recorded
Links
F36, FR35, C75

An email account is taken over

S85 · digital channels
How it starts
A mailbox without strong sign-in is used to send or intercept payment instructions.
Why it matters
Most business email compromise starts with an account that has no multi-factor authentication.
What the answers show
Multi-factor on all email: email accounts are protected by passwords only.
What would interrupt it
Multi-factor authentication is required for every email account, including executives' and shared mailboxes, with no exceptions.
Warning signs
Takeover of staff accounts; Business email compromise and payment redirection
Links
F46, FR45, C90
