    DOMAIN OVERVIEW




Domain overview

      The systemic pattern
      The underlying condition that links the priority findings is an absence of process-level ownership, consistent control
      evidence and repeatable monitoring. Fraud risks are not yet mapped and signed by process owners in a way that ties
      scenarios to the preventive and detective controls that must operate day-to-day (the relevant control). Where supplier
      onboarding and bank verification are partly designed (the relevant control), and role-based and privileged access
      governance remain informal or inconsistent (the relevant control, the relevant control), the organisation lacks the
      authoritative facts and decisive challenge needed to stop value being redirected at supplier- and payment-change points.
      At the same time, analytics and rule-based detection remain partly designed (the relevant control), so exceptions do not
      reliably surface for accountable review, and evidence preservation and chain-of-custody handling are informal (the
      relevant control), weakening containment when concerns are raised. The relative strength in whistleblowing and reporting
      (D6) matters only if intake is routed into a reliable containment and investigative path; presently reporting capacity is not
      consistently converted into preserved evidence, mapped scenarios or tuned monitoring that forces remediation. In short,
      the causal chain runs from incomplete fraud-risk mapping through inconsistent supplier and access controls to limited
      detection and fragile incident containment — and that chain is the priority management question to close.

    PRIORITY FINDINGS, CONTRADICTIONS AND SCENARIOS




Priority findings, contradictions and scenarios
Role distinction: Control owner identifies the role responsible for implementing or operating the control. Accountable executive identifies the executive role
responsible for sponsorship, decision rights and escalation. The two roles may legitimately differ.



      Value diversion through supplier and payment processes
      Supplier-onboarding verification is partly designed and important elements are incomplete or inconsistent (the relevant
      control). Without complete, pre-activation verification of legal identity, ownership and bank details and a second
      reviewer’s approval, supplier activation and payment processes remain exposed to diversion or fictitious relationships.
      Management implication: enforce an independent pre-activation verification and second-review requirement so no
      vendor or bank detail is activated in the ERP without a retained evidence package and independent sign-off.

      Weak challenge at identity, transaction and sensitive-change points
      Analytics and rule-based detection are partly designed (the relevant control), and role- and privileged-access governance
      is either partly designed or informal (the relevant control, the relevant control). When monitoring tests, exception
      ownership and access recertification are not consistently in place, unusual transactions, beneficiary changes and sensitive
      system changes may not receive timely challenge. Management implication: implement a defined monitoring catalogue, a
      named reviewer for exceptions and a strict recertification rhythm for sensitive access so that unusual activity is escalated
      and constrained before value is redirected.

      Limited containment and learning after suspected fraud
      Evidence preservation and chain-of-custody handling were self-assessed as informal or reactive (the relevant control). If
      suspected matters are not opened into an evidence register, hashed or sealed and placed in an access-controlled
      repository with a recorded custody trail, investigations can be delayed or undermined and learning loops remain closed.
      Management implication: define and operate an intake, preservation and custody route for every material case so that
      reporting becomes an input to containment and improvement rather than an unresolved alert.

      Additional material control pattern
      Several control gaps that are material but distinct from the three exposures above were identified: privileged access
      governance needs a complete sensitive-access inventory and quarterly recertification (the relevant control); role-based
      access must be aligned to current role requirements and segregation-of-duties exceptions managed (the relevant control);
      conflicts of interest involving suppliers are managed informally and should be formalised (the relevant control); and the
      workforce’s ability to use reporting channels is partly designed and needs clearer guidance and testing (the relevant
      control). Management implication: treat privileged and ordinary access governance and third-party conflict declarations as
      discrete workstreams with named owners and completion criteria so these controls cease to be informal dependencies.

Scenarios and assurance tests
Conditional exposure pathways
The scenarios below are conditional illustrations of how the linked weaknesses could combine if not treated; they are
management tests of prevention, detection and containment.

SUPPLIER OR PAYMENT INSTRUCTION IS DIVERTED THROUGH A COMPROMISED OR FICTITIOUS RELATIONSHIP

This pathway can begin when supplier onboarding and bank-detail changes are processed without consistent independent
verification (SCENARIO-001). The current environment could fail if an outsourced supplier-management step accepts a change
shortly before payment and there is no pre-activation evidence pack or second reviewer blocking ERP activation. Warning
indicators include a supplier activated before verification, a bank-detail change shortly before payment, or an unresolved
ownership/conflict match. Immediate containment is to pause the affected supplier or payment instruction, independently
confirm the trusted beneficiary details and preserve the approval trail. Longer-term response is to implement independent
supplier and bank-detail verification, dual approval and continuous vendor-population monitoring so activation and payment
cannot proceed without the evidence package.

PRIVILEGED ACCESS IS USED TO ALTER A VALUE-BEARING RECORD, ENTITLEMENT OR AUDIT TRAIL

This pathway can begin where privileged or administrator accounts remain usable across locations without consistent
recertification (SCENARIO-002). The environment could fail if sensitive access is not fully inventoried, strongly authenticated,
session-logged and recertified quarterly. Warning indicators include missed recertifications, unknown privileged accounts, leaver
access remaining beyond the defined window, or unusual entitlement changes. Immediate containment is to suspend or restrict
the affected entitlement, preserve access and audit logs, and route the change for accountable review. Longer-term response is
implementing least-privilege role-based access, privileged-session logging, quarterly independent recertification and event-driven
removal processes.

RECORDS OR REPORTING ARE WEAKENED AFTER A SUSPECTED FRAUD MATTER, ALLOWING EXPOSURE TO REPEAT

This pathway can begin when a suspected matter originates at a site and records remain uncontrolled at the local level because
evidence preservation and custody are informal (SCENARIO-003). The environment could fail if no evidence register is opened,
integrity checks are absent and custody transfers are unrecorded, making scope and timing harder to establish when the matter
is escalated. Warning indicators include unexplained custody gaps, unavailable records or overdue severity decisions. Immediate
containment is to open an incident record, preserve relevant records under controlled custody and assign a named containment
and investigation owner. Longer-term response is to implement protected reporting, severity-based escalation, formal evidence
preservation and repeat-exposure review so suspected matters are contained and converted into action.

E2. Definitions and score basis
How the score is calculated
Each applicable assessment response is normalised to a 0–100 control score and weighted within its domain. Domain scores are
then combined using the methodology's domain weights to produce the overall readiness score. A control recorded as not
applicable is excluded from its scoring denominator rather than treated as a weakness.

The weighted overall score is 43.33/100, which maps numerically to Developing. The final maturity shown in this report is
Developing. The next numerical band begins at 60/100; a higher numerical score alone does not override a stricter applicable
maturity ceiling.

Maturity scale

   MATURITY                                     SCORE RANGE                  INTERPRETATION


   Reactive                                     0–<40                        Foundational controls are limited or mainly reactive.


   Developing                                   40–<60                       Core control elements exist but are incomplete, inconsistent or not yet connected.


   Structured                                   60–<80                       Controls are more consistently defined, owned and operated.


   Strategic                                    80–100                       Fraud-risk management is embedded, monitored and continually improved.

Maturity ceilings
Maturity ceilings protect against a strong average masking a material weakness. A hard-gate critical control scored 0 or 1 sets a
maximum of Developing; a hard-gate control scored 2 sets a maximum of Structured; three or more critical controls scored 0, 1
or 2 set a maximum of Developing; any core domain below 40 sets a maximum of Developing; and any core domain below 60
sets a maximum of Structured. Where several ceilings apply, the strictest ceiling governs the final maturity.
